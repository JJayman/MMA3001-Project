treeskel.measure_registration

Measure how far the depth images sit from the colour images (their registration).

Session 8 found that the winter depth images sit about 4 px below the colour images (docs/results/depth_registration/). The tracing follows depth, so the traced paths sit about 1 px off the bark on sloping leaders, and every check of the masks against depth compared them with a shifted silhouette. This module measures the shift on development-row trees only (the test rows of splits.json are left out), so that the correction (treeskel.io.DEPTH_SHIFT) is not chosen on test data. The depth is loaded as stored (register=False), unless --registered asks for a check of the correction itself. Shifts are written (dy, dx): where the depth sits relative to the colour, so positive dy means the depth is lower. The correction moves the depth by about (−dy, −dx).

  1. Across the leaders (winter; the reference). Along each traced leader, colour gives the bark's two edges (treeskel.guided_gt.branch_edges()) and the depth silhouette gives the ends of its run through the same cross-section (treeskel.measure_width.centre_runs()). If the images were registered, the run's centre would sit on the bark's centre, whatever the branch's direction; a shift s of the depth moves it by n · s along the cross-section's normal n. The difference of the centres is therefore fitted as n · s by robust (Huber) least squares (fit_shift()), on cross-sections clear of the other leader whose depth run is closed on both sides (leader_sections()). Depth's overshoot past the bark's edges cancels, being the same on both sides. The fit is made pooled over the trees, per orchard row, per tree, in bands across the image, and with an affine term (fit_affine(): does the shift change across the image?).

    The depth is then moved by each shift on a grid (TRIAL_MOVES), with the traced paths kept, and the shift is fitted again; the move that leaves no fitted shift, interpolated over the grid (zero_move()), estimates the correction. The fit on the cross-sections usable at every move, and the move at which depth reaches equally far past the bark's upper and lower edges on sloping leaders, are reported too. Each tree's leaders are also split into the halves near and far from the fork, fitted separately: if trees really differ in their shift, the two halves agree tree by tree (split_half()).

    The colour centre follows its window (found in session 9). On real leaders the bark's centre found in colour moves with the window it is measured in: moving the window 1 px along the normal moves the mean centre 0.55–0.7 px (the median does not move: most cross-sections are unaffected, some jump as clutter enters or leaves the window's ends; on synthetic bark the pull is at most 0.2, even blurred or on a dark background). Robust fits and a test of each cross-section's stability did not remove it. The windows sit on the traced paths, which follow the depth, so the estimate above depends on the paths: traced again with the depth moved up 4 rows, the same images read about 0.9 px more. The correction is therefore also estimated in ways that the pull affects less (correction_estimates()): on cross-sections whose two edges are both clear (a grey-level change over CONTRAST; the pull there is about 0.4), with the colour measured on the depth run's centre instead of the path (anchored_differences(): zero where the depth lines up, whatever the pull, but it magnifies any bias of the colour centre of its own), and by tracing each tree again with the depth moved by CHECK_MOVES and interpolating the shift left. Each is made for all trees and for each orchard row; the rows' spread gives its uncertainty. On the 21 local trees these gave 3.7 (paths kept) to 5.8 rows up (the colour on the depth run).

  2. Mutual information (both seasons). The summer images have no traced branches, so (1) cannot be used there, and a check along single depth edges was tried and failed on winter (thin, shaded bark gives no clear colour step at one edge; it read 0.9 px where (1) reads 3.2, and moved 0.2–1 px for a 3–4 px move). Mutual information, the usual measure for registering images from different sensors, needs neither: for each shift within MI_REACH px, the information the depth (no reading, then bins of DEPTH_STEP levels) carries about the colour (each channel in COLOUR_LEVELS levels) is computed over the image and over bands of rows (mi_surface()), and the shift with the most, refined between pixels (mi_peak()), is taken. Surfaces are summed over trees for pooled estimates. It is checked against (1) on winter: on 21 trees the whole-image estimates were 4.0 and 3.2–3.8 px down, but the top band of rows read 1.5 px where (1) read 3.5, single trees did not agree with (1) at all (r = +0.01), and a tree's surface can rise towards the edge of the search (the far ground's range limit is a depth edge with no colour edge). So mutual information is reported by band and pooled over many trees only.

 177def edge_contrast(grey: np.ndarray, edge: np.ndarray, outward: int, reach: int = 3) -> np.ndarray:
 178    """Change of the mean grey level across an edge of each profile: ``reach`` samples inside against outside.
 179
 180    Args:
 181        grey: ``(N, 2X + 1)`` grey profiles centred on the path.
 182        edge: ``(N,)`` edge offsets (px, from :func:`treeskel.guided_gt.profile_edges`).
 183        outward: −1 for the ``first`` edge, +1 for the ``last``.
 184        reach: Samples averaged each side, as in :func:`treeskel.measure_width.edge_overshoot`.
 185
 186    Returns:
 187        ``(N,)`` absolute differences of the mean grey levels.
 188    """
 189    half, width = grey.shape[1] // 2, grey.shape[1]
 190    rows = np.arange(len(grey))[:, None]
 191    inside = np.clip(half + edge[:, None] - outward * np.arange(reach)[None], 0, width - 1)
 192    outside = np.clip(half + edge[:, None] + outward * np.arange(1, reach + 1)[None], 0, width - 1)
 193    return np.abs(grey[rows, inside].mean(1) - grey[rows, outside].mean(1))

 196def leader_sections(rgb: np.ndarray, gt: WinterGT, half_width: int | None = None) -> dict[str, np.ndarray]:
 197    """The leaders' cross-sections that can be compared with depth, and their colour edges.
 198
 199    The edges are found as :func:`treeskel.guided_gt.branch_edges` finds
 200    them. Cross-sections that could reach the other leader
 201    (:func:`treeskel.guided_gt.clear_of`) or whose bark is narrower than
 202    :data:`MIN_HALF_WIDTH` are left out.
 203
 204    Args:
 205        rgb: The winter colour image.
 206        gt: The traced tree (:func:`treeskel.guided_gt.guided_gt`).
 207        half_width: Reach of each cross-section either side of the path (px);
 208            default :attr:`treeskel.guided_gt.GuidedParams.profile_half_width`.
 209
 210    Returns:
 211        Arrays, one entry per cross-section: ``side`` (0 left, 1 right),
 212        ``index`` (the point on that leader's path), ``frac`` (its distance
 213        along the leader, as a fraction of the leader's length), ``row`` and
 214        ``col``, the normal ``n_row`` and ``n_col``
 215        (:func:`treeskel.guided_gt.path_normals`), the colour edges
 216        ``first`` and ``last`` (offsets along the normal, px), and ``clear``:
 217        True where the grey level changes by more than :data:`CONTRAST`
 218        across both edges (:func:`edge_contrast`).
 219    """
 220    half = half_width or GuidedParams().profile_half_width
 221    along = GuidedParams().profile_along
 222    parts = []
 223    for k, (side, other) in enumerate((("left", gt.right), ("right", gt.left))):
 224        path = getattr(gt, side)
 225        if len(path) == 0:
 226            continue
 227        samples = straighten(rgb, path, half)
 228        if along > 0:
 229            samples = ndi.uniform_filter1d(samples, 2 * along + 1, axis=0, mode="nearest")
 230        first, last = profile_edges(samples)
 231        grey = samples.mean(-1)
 232        clear = (edge_contrast(grey, first, -1) > CONTRAST) & (edge_contrast(grey, last, 1) > CONTRAST)
 233        use = clear_of(path, other, half + NEIGHBOUR_MARGIN) & ((last - first + 1) / 2.0 > MIN_HALF_WIDTH)
 234        s, n = arc_length(path), path_normals(path)
 235        i = np.nonzero(use)[0]
 236        parts.append({"side": np.full(len(i), k), "index": i, "frac": s[i] / max(s[-1], 1.0),
 237                      "row": path[i, 0], "col": path[i, 1], "n_row": n[i, 0], "n_col": n[i, 1],
 238                      "first": first[i], "last": last[i], "clear": clear[i]})
 239    keys = ("side", "index", "frac", "row", "col", "n_row", "n_col", "first", "last", "clear")
 240    return {k: np.concatenate([p[k] for p in parts]) if parts else np.zeros(0, bool if k == "clear" else float)
 241            for k in keys}

 244def depth_runs(fg: np.ndarray, gt: WinterGT, sections: dict[str, np.ndarray],
 245               half_width: int | None = None) -> tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray, np.ndarray]:
 246    """Ends of the depth silhouette's run through each cross-section.
 247
 248    Args:
 249        fg: ``(H, W)`` bool depth silhouette (:func:`silhouette`).
 250        gt: The traced tree whose leaders the sections lie on.
 251        sections: From :func:`leader_sections`.
 252        half_width: As in :func:`leader_sections`.
 253
 254    Returns:
 255        ``(d0, d1, closed)`` per cross-section, as in
 256        :func:`treeskel.measure_width.centre_runs`.
 257    """
 258    half = half_width or GuidedParams().profile_half_width
 259    d0 = np.zeros(len(sections["side"]), int)
 260    d1 = np.zeros(len(sections["side"]), int)
 261    closed = np.zeros(len(sections["side"]), bool)
 262    for k, side in enumerate(("left", "right")):
 263        mine = sections["side"] == k
 264        if not mine.any():
 265            continue
 266        path = getattr(gt, side)
 267        a, b, c = centre_runs(straighten(fg.astype(float), path, half, order=0) > 0.5)
 268        i = sections["index"][mine].astype(int)
 269        d0[mine], d1[mine], closed[mine] = a[i], b[i], c[i]
 270    return d0, d1, closed

 273def centre_difference(first, last, d0, d1) -> np.ndarray:
 274    """The depth run's centre minus the bark's centre, along each cross-section's normal (px).
 275
 276    Args:
 277        first: Colour edge offsets per cross-section (px).
 278        last: The other colour edge's offsets (px).
 279        d0: The depth run's first offsets (px).
 280        d1: Its last offsets (px).
 281
 282    Returns:
 283        ``(N,)`` differences (px).
 284    """
 285    return (np.asarray(d0) + np.asarray(d1)) / 2.0 - (np.asarray(first) + np.asarray(last)) / 2.0

 288def anchored_differences(rgb: np.ndarray, fg: np.ndarray, gt: WinterGT, sections: dict[str, np.ndarray],
 289                         half_width: int | None = None) -> tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray]:
 290    """The depth run's centre minus the bark's centre, with the colour measured on the depth run's centre.
 291
 292    On real leaders the bark's centre found in colour follows the window it
 293    is measured in: with the window centred on ``w``, it comes out near
 294    ``b + k · (w − b)`` for a bark centred on ``b`` (``k`` about 0.5 on the
 295    development trees). Centred on the path, the measured difference then
 296    depends on the path. Centred on the depth run's centre ``d`` (which does
 297    not depend on the path), the difference is ``(1 − k) · (d − b)``:
 298    smaller, but zero exactly where the depth lines up with the colour, so
 299    the move of the depth that leaves no shift is the registration whatever
 300    ``k`` is. Any bias of the colour centre of its own is magnified by
 301    ``1 / (1 − k)``, though.
 302
 303    Args:
 304        rgb: The winter colour image.
 305        fg: The depth silhouette (:func:`silhouette`).
 306        gt: The traced tree; its paths only place the cross-sections.
 307        sections: From :func:`leader_sections` on ``gt``.
 308        half_width: As in :func:`leader_sections`.
 309
 310    Returns:
 311        ``(delta, usable)`` per cross-section: the difference (px, along the
 312        normal) and whether the depth run through it is closed.
 313    """
 314    half = half_width or GuidedParams().profile_half_width
 315    along = GuidedParams().profile_along
 316    delta = np.zeros(len(sections["side"]))
 317    usable = np.zeros(len(sections["side"]), bool)
 318    image = rgb.astype(float)
 319    offsets = np.arange(-half, half + 1)[None, :]
 320    for k, side in enumerate(("left", "right")):
 321        mine = sections["side"] == k
 322        if not mine.any():
 323            continue
 324        path = getattr(gt, side)
 325        n = path_normals(path)
 326        d0, d1, closed = centre_runs(straighten(fg.astype(float), path, half, order=0) > 0.5)
 327        anchor = path + np.where(closed, (d0 + d1) / 2.0, 0.0)[:, None] * n
 328        coords = [anchor[:, :1] + offsets * n[:, :1], anchor[:, 1:] + offsets * n[:, 1:]]
 329        samples = np.stack([ndi.map_coordinates(image[..., c], coords, order=1, mode="nearest")
 330                            for c in range(image.shape[2])], -1)
 331        if along > 0:
 332            samples = ndi.uniform_filter1d(samples, 2 * along + 1, axis=0, mode="nearest")
 333        first, last = profile_edges(samples)
 334        i = sections["index"][mine].astype(int)
 335        delta[mine] = -(first[i] + last[i]) / 2.0
 336        usable[mine] = closed[i]
 337    return delta, usable

 340def edge_overshoots(n_row, first, last, d0, d1) -> tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray]:
 341    """How far the depth run reaches past the bark's upper and lower edges (px, outwards positive).
 342
 343    The normal points along increasing offsets, so where it points up
 344    (``n_row < 0``) the ``last`` edge is the upper one.
 345
 346    Args:
 347        n_row: Each cross-section normal's row component.
 348        first: Colour edge offsets per cross-section (px).
 349        last: The other colour edge's offsets (px).
 350        d0: The depth run's first offsets (px).
 351        d1: Its last offsets (px).
 352
 353    Returns:
 354        ``(upper, lower)``: ``(N,)`` overshoots (px) past each edge.
 355    """
 356    past_first = np.asarray(first) - np.asarray(d0)
 357    past_last = np.asarray(d1) - np.asarray(last)
 358    up = np.asarray(n_row) < 0
 359    return np.where(up, past_last, past_first), np.where(up, past_first, past_last)

 364def huber_fit(X: np.ndarray, y: np.ndarray, k: float = 1.345, iters: int = 40) -> np.ndarray:
 365    """Robust linear least squares ``y ≈ X @ c`` by Huber's M-estimator.
 366
 367    Iteratively reweighted: residuals within ``k`` robust standard deviations
 368    (1.4826 × the median absolute deviation of the residuals from their
 369    median) get full weight, larger ones ``k·σ/|r|``.
 370
 371    Args:
 372        X: ``(n, p)`` design matrix.
 373        y: ``(n,)`` values.
 374        k: Huber's tuning constant (in robust SDs).
 375        iters: Most reweighting rounds.
 376
 377    Returns:
 378        ``c``; zeros if ``y`` is empty.
 379    """
 380    X, y = np.asarray(X, float), np.asarray(y, float)
 381    if len(y) == 0:
 382        return np.zeros(X.shape[1] if X.ndim == 2 else 1)
 383    w = np.ones(len(y))
 384    c = np.zeros(X.shape[1])
 385    for _ in range(iters):
 386        sw = np.sqrt(w)
 387        c, *_ = np.linalg.lstsq(X * sw[:, None], y * sw, rcond=None)
 388        r = y - X @ c
 389        s = 1.4826 * np.median(np.abs(r - np.median(r))) or 1.0
 390        w = np.where(np.abs(r) <= k * s, 1.0, k * s / np.maximum(np.abs(r), 1e-12))
 391    return c

 394def fit_shift(n_row, n_col, delta) -> tuple[float, float]:
 395    """The fixed image shift ``(dy, dx)`` of the depth that best explains the centre differences.
 396
 397    Each cross-section's centre difference (depth minus colour, along its
 398    normal) is modelled as ``n_row · dy + n_col · dx`` and fitted by
 399    :func:`huber_fit`. Normals in both directions are needed for both parts:
 400    vertical branches say nothing about ``dy``.
 401
 402    Args:
 403        n_row: Each cross-section normal's row component.
 404        n_col: Each normal's column component.
 405        delta: Each cross-section's centre difference (px).
 406
 407    Returns:
 408        ``(dy, dx)`` in px; nan if there are fewer than 3 cross-sections.
 409    """
 410    if len(delta) < 3:
 411        return float("nan"), float("nan")
 412    dy, dx = huber_fit(np.c_[n_row, n_col], delta)
 413    return float(dy), float(dx)

 416def fit_affine(n_row, n_col, row, col, delta, shape: tuple[int, int] = (480, 640)) -> dict[str, float]:
 417    """A shift that may change linearly across the image: ``s + A · (p − centre)``.
 418
 419    ``A`` holds scale, rotation and shear. It is reported as the change of
 420    each part of the shift from the image's centre to its bottom and right
 421    edges (px), so a pure shift gives zeros.
 422
 423    Args:
 424        n_row: Each cross-section normal's row component.
 425        n_col: Each normal's column component.
 426        row: Each cross-section's image row.
 427        col: Each cross-section's image column.
 428        delta: Each cross-section's centre difference (px).
 429        shape: Image shape ``(H, W)``.
 430
 431    Returns:
 432        ``{"dy", "dx", "dy_to_bottom", "dy_to_right", "dx_to_bottom", "dx_to_right"}``.
 433    """
 434    h, w = shape
 435    y, x = (np.asarray(row) - h / 2) / (h / 2), (np.asarray(col) - w / 2) / (w / 2)
 436    X = np.c_[n_row, n_col, n_row * y, n_row * x, n_col * y, n_col * x]
 437    c = huber_fit(X, delta)
 438    return dict(zip(("dy", "dx", "dy_to_bottom", "dy_to_right", "dx_to_bottom", "dx_to_right"), map(float, c)))

 441def zero_move(moves: Sequence[tuple[int, int]], shifts: Sequence[tuple[float, float]]) -> dict:
 442    """The move of the depth that leaves no fitted shift, from fits at several moves.
 443
 444    The remaining shift is modelled as linear in the move,
 445    ``r(m) = r0 + J · m``, fitted by least squares over the moves; for a
 446    measure that follows a move one for one, ``J`` is the identity. The move
 447    that leaves none is ``−J⁻¹ · r0``.
 448
 449    Args:
 450        moves: ``(rows, cols)`` the depth was moved by.
 451        shifts: The fitted ``(dy, dx)`` remaining at each move (nan ones are skipped).
 452
 453    Returns:
 454        ``{"move": [rows, cols], "response": J, "at_no_move": r0}``, or None
 455        with fewer than 3 usable moves.
 456    """
 457    m, r = np.asarray(moves, float), np.asarray(shifts, float)
 458    ok = np.isfinite(r).all(1)
 459    if ok.sum() < 3:
 460        return None
 461    X = np.c_[np.ones(ok.sum()), m[ok]]
 462    coef, *_ = np.linalg.lstsq(X, r[ok], rcond=None)           # rows: r0, then J's columns
 463    r0, jac = coef[0], coef[1:].T
 464    return {"move": [float(v) for v in -np.linalg.solve(jac, r0)], "response": jac.round(3).tolist(),
 465            "at_no_move": [float(v) for v in r0]}

 468def split_half(first: np.ndarray, second: np.ndarray) -> dict:
 469    """How much of the spread of per-tree estimates is real, from two independent halves per tree.
 470
 471    If each tree has its own shift ``t`` and each half measures it with
 472    independent noise, the halves' covariance across trees is the variance of
 473    ``t`` and their correlation is the share of the spread that is real.
 474
 475    Args:
 476        first: One estimate per tree from the first half (nan skipped).
 477        second: The same trees' estimates from the second half.
 478
 479    Returns:
 480        ``{"n_trees", "pearson", "spearman", "real_sd", "noise_sd"}``: the
 481        standard deviation across trees of the real shift (from the
 482        covariance; 0 if it is negative) and of one half's noise.
 483    """
 484    a, b = np.asarray(first, float), np.asarray(second, float)
 485    ok = np.isfinite(a) & np.isfinite(b)
 486    a, b = a[ok], b[ok]
 487    if len(a) < 5:
 488        return {"n_trees": int(len(a)), "pearson": None, "spearman": None, "real_sd": None, "noise_sd": None}
 489    cov = float(np.cov(a, b)[0, 1])
 490    noise = float(np.sqrt(max(np.var(a - b, ddof=1) / 2, 0.0)))
 491    return {"n_trees": int(len(a)), "pearson": float(np.corrcoef(a, b)[0, 1]),
 492            "spearman": float(spearmanr(a, b).statistic), "real_sd": float(np.sqrt(max(cov, 0.0))),
 493            "noise_sd": noise}

 498def _mutual_information(joint: np.ndarray) -> float:
 499    """Mutual information (nats) of a joint histogram."""
 500    total = joint.sum()
 501    if total == 0:
 502        return 0.0
 503    p = joint / total
 504    pa, pb = p.sum(1, keepdims=True), p.sum(0, keepdims=True)
 505    nz = p > 0
 506    return float((p[nz] * np.log(p[nz] / (pa @ pb)[nz])).sum())

 509def mi_surface(rgb: np.ndarray, depth: np.ndarray, valid: np.ndarray, reach: int = MI_REACH,
 510               bands: Sequence[tuple[int, int]] = ROW_BANDS, levels: int = COLOUR_LEVELS,
 511               step: int = DEPTH_STEP, margin: int | None = None) -> np.ndarray:
 512    """Mutual information between the colour image and the depth, for each shift of the depth.
 513
 514    Entry ``[b, reach + dy, reach + dx]`` compares the colour at ``x`` with the
 515    depth at ``x + (dy, dx)``, so the shift with the most information is where
 516    the depth sits relative to the colour. Every shift uses the same colour
 517    pixels: all but a margin. The colour is coded with ``levels`` levels per
 518    channel; the depth as no reading, or in bins of ``step`` levels.
 519
 520    Args:
 521        rgb: One tree's ``(480, 640, 3)`` uint8 photo, for one season.
 522        depth: Its ``(480, 640)`` uint8 depth.
 523        valid: Boolean mask of the depth's readings.
 524        reach: Largest shift tried each way (px).
 525        bands: Row ranges measured on their own as well.
 526        levels: Levels per colour channel in the colour's code.
 527        step: Depth values per bin of the depth's code (no reading is a bin of its own).
 528        margin: Colour pixels this close to the edge are not compared (at
 529            least ``reach``, the default). A depth moved on loading has an
 530            edge of "no reading" where the move uncovered it; paired with the
 531            colour there it makes a peak of its own, so the margin should
 532            then exceed ``reach`` by the move.
 533
 534    Returns:
 535        ``(1 + len(bands), 2·reach + 1, 2·reach + 1)``: the whole image, then each band.
 536    """
 537    h, w = depth.shape
 538    m = max(reach, margin or 0)
 539    q = rgb.astype(int) * levels // 256
 540    colour = ((q[..., 0] * levels + q[..., 1]) * levels + q[..., 2])[m:h - m, m:w - m]
 541    n_depth = 255 // step + 2
 542    coded = np.where(valid, 1 + depth.astype(int) // step, 0)
 543    # Rows of each band inside the margin, relative to the compared region.
 544    spans = [(max(lo, m) - m, max(min(hi, h - m) - m, max(lo, m) - m)) for lo, hi in bands]
 545    # If the bands tile the compared rows, the whole image's histogram is the sum of theirs.
 546    tiled = bool(spans) and spans[0][0] == 0 and spans[-1][1] == h - 2 * m and all(
 547        a[1] == b[0] for a, b in zip(spans, spans[1:]))
 548    size = levels ** 3 * n_depth
 549    out = np.zeros((1 + len(bands), 2 * reach + 1, 2 * reach + 1))
 550    for i, dy in enumerate(range(-reach, reach + 1)):
 551        for j, dx in enumerate(range(-reach, reach + 1)):
 552            joint = colour * n_depth + coded[m + dy:h - m + dy, m + dx:w - m + dx]
 553            parts = [np.bincount(joint[a:b].ravel(), minlength=size) for a, b in spans]
 554            whole = sum(parts) if tiled else np.bincount(joint.ravel(), minlength=size)
 555            out[0, i, j] = _mutual_information(whole.reshape(-1, n_depth))
 556            for k, part in enumerate(parts):
 557                out[1 + k, i, j] = _mutual_information(part.reshape(-1, n_depth))
 558    return out

 561def mi_peak(surface: np.ndarray, window: int = PEAK_WINDOW, iters: int = 5) -> tuple[float, float, bool]:
 562    """The centre of the peak of mutual information, between pixels.
 563
 564    Depth reaches past the edges of what it sees (by about 2 px), so every
 565    shift that keeps the colour's objects inside the depth's scores about the
 566    same: the peak is a plateau, and its highest point is decided by noise.
 567    On synthetic scenes whose depth overshoots by 2 px, the highest point
 568    missed the true shift by up to 1.5 px (a parabola through it and its
 569    neighbours did not help). So a quadratic surface is fitted by least
 570    squares to the ``(2·window + 1)²`` values around the highest point, and
 571    the window is moved to the vertex and fitted again until it settles;
 572    the vertex is the estimate. On the same scenes it was within 0.15 px.
 573
 574    Args:
 575        surface: ``(2·reach + 1, 2·reach + 1)``, one entry of :func:`mi_surface` (or a sum).
 576        window: Half-size of the fitted window.
 577        iters: Most refits.
 578
 579    Returns:
 580        ``(dy, dx, at_edge)``; ``at_edge`` is True if the window could not be
 581        centred on the peak (it lies within ``window`` of the edge of the
 582        search, or beyond it) or the fit has no maximum, when the peak may lie
 583        further out than was searched. The shift is kept within the search
 584        (on all 449 development trees, one row's winter surface extrapolated
 585        to 113 px).
 586    """
 587    size = surface.shape[0]
 588    reach = size // 2
 589    w = min(window, reach)
 590    # Start from the top of the lightly smoothed surface: the highest single value wanders on a plateau.
 591    smooth = ndi.gaussian_filter(np.asarray(surface, float), 1.0, mode="nearest")
 592    peak = np.array(np.unravel_index(int(np.argmax(smooth)), surface.shape), float)
 593    offsets = np.mgrid[-w:w + 1, -w:w + 1].reshape(2, -1).T.astype(float)
 594    design = np.c_[np.ones(len(offsets)), offsets, offsets ** 2, offsets[:, 0] * offsets[:, 1]]
 595    ok = True
 596    for _ in range(iters):
 597        centre = np.clip(np.round(peak).astype(int), w, size - 1 - w)
 598        z = surface[centre[0] - w:centre[0] + w + 1, centre[1] - w:centre[1] + w + 1].ravel()
 599        c, *_ = np.linalg.lstsq(design, z, rcond=None)
 600        hess = np.array([[2 * c[3], c[5]], [c[5], 2 * c[4]]])
 601        if np.linalg.eigvalsh(hess).max() >= 0:                        # no maximum: keep the highest point
 602            ok = False
 603            break
 604        new = centre + np.linalg.solve(hess, -c[1:3])
 605        if np.allclose(new, peak, atol=1e-3):
 606            peak = new
 607            break
 608        peak = new
 609    edge = not ok or bool(np.any(np.abs(peak - reach) > reach - w))
 610    # A vertex far outside the fitted window means nothing: report at most the edge of the search.
 611    peak = np.clip(peak, 0, size - 1)
 612    return float(peak[0] - reach), float(peak[1] - reach), edge

 617def measure_tree(job: tuple) -> dict:
 618    """All measurements for one tree (top-level so worker processes can run it).
 619
 620    Args:
 621        job: ``(tree_record, clicks, registered)``; ``registered`` loads the
 622            depth moved by :data:`treeskel.io.DEPTH_SHIFT` (to check the
 623            correction) instead of as stored.
 624
 625    Returns:
 626        The tree's cross-sections (:func:`leader_sections`); at every move in
 627        :data:`TRIAL_MOVES`, the depth runs through them and the differences
 628        measured on the depth run's centre (:func:`anchored_differences`);
 629        the cross-sections of the tree traced again with the depth moved by
 630        each of :data:`CHECK_MOVES` (not when ``registered``: the tree is
 631        then traced with the corrected depth already); the median depth
 632        along the leaders, the capture time, and the mutual-information
 633        surfaces of both seasons.
 634    """
 635    from treeskel.io import DEPTH_SHIFT, load_tree
 636
 637    def compact(sec: dict) -> dict:
 638        """The cross-sections in smaller number types, to keep the saved file small."""
 639        return {k: v if v.dtype == bool else v.astype(np.float32 if v.dtype.kind == "f" else np.int16)
 640                for k, v in sec.items()}
 641
 642    rec, clicks, registered = job
 643    winter = load_tree(rec, "winter", register=registered)
 644    gt = guided_gt(winter["rgb"], winter["depth"], winter["valid"], clicks)
 645    sections = leader_sections(winter["rgb"], gt)
 646    runs, anchored = [], []
 647    for move in TRIAL_MOVES:
 648        depth = shift_image(winter["depth"], move)
 649        fg = silhouette(depth, depth > 0)
 650        runs.append(depth_runs(fg, gt, sections))
 651        anchored.append(anchored_differences(winter["rgb"], fg, gt, sections))
 652    checks = []
 653    for move in () if registered else CHECK_MOVES:
 654        # Traced again with the moved depth, as the ground truth will be after a correction.
 655        depth = shift_image(winter["depth"], move)
 656        again = guided_gt(winter["rgb"], depth, depth > 0, clicks)
 657        sec = leader_sections(winter["rgb"], again)
 658        a, b, c = depth_runs(silhouette(depth, depth > 0), again, sec)
 659        checks.append({"move": list(move), "sections": compact(sec), "d0": a.astype(np.int8), "d1": b.astype(np.int8),
 660                       "closed": c})
 661    along = np.concatenate([gt.left, gt.right])
 662    values = winter["depth"][along[:, 0], along[:, 1]]
 663    summer = load_tree(rec, "summer", register=registered)
 664    # The same colour pixels with the depth as stored or moved: a margin beyond the largest move.
 665    margin = MI_REACH + max(abs(v) for shift in DEPTH_SHIFT.values() for v in shift)
 666    return {
 667        "tree": rec.tree_id, "row": rec.row,
 668        "time": parse_filename(rec.paths[("winter", "depth")]).time,
 669        "sections": compact(sections),
 670        "d0": np.array([r[0] for r in runs], np.int8), "d1": np.array([r[1] for r in runs], np.int8),
 671        "closed": np.array([r[2] for r in runs], bool),
 672        "anchored": np.array([a[0] for a in anchored], np.float32),
 673        "anchored_ok": np.array([a[1] for a in anchored], bool),
 674        "checks": checks,
 675        "depth_value": float(np.median(values[values > 0])) if (values > 0).any() else float("nan"),
 676        "mi": {season: mi_surface(d["rgb"], d["depth"], d["valid"], margin=margin).astype(np.float32)
 677               for season, d in (("winter", winter), ("summer", summer))},
 678    }

 683def _pool(results: list[dict], move: int, keep=None, anchored: bool = False) -> dict[str, np.ndarray]:
 684    """All trees' cross-sections usable at one move (an index into :data:`TRIAL_MOVES`).
 685
 686    Args:
 687        results: :func:`measure_tree` results.
 688        move: Index of the move of the depth.
 689        keep: Per tree, which cross-sections to use (default: those whose
 690            depth run is closed at this move).
 691        anchored: Use the differences measured on the depth run's centre
 692            (:func:`anchored_differences`) instead of on the path.
 693    """
 694    parts = []
 695    for t, r in enumerate(results):
 696        s = r["sections"]
 697        if keep is not None:
 698            use = keep[t]
 699        else:
 700            use = r["anchored_ok"][move] if anchored else r["closed"][move]
 701        first, last, d0, d1 = s["first"][use], s["last"][use], r["d0"][move][use], r["d1"][move][use]
 702        parts.append({"tree": np.full(int(use.sum()), t), "n_row": s["n_row"][use], "n_col": s["n_col"][use],
 703                      "row": s["row"][use], "col": s["col"][use], "frac": s["frac"][use], "clear": s["clear"][use],
 704                      "delta": r["anchored"][move][use] if anchored else centre_difference(first, last, d0, d1),
 705                      "upper_lower": np.stack(edge_overshoots(s["n_row"][use], first, last, d0, d1), -1)})
 706    keys = ("tree", "n_row", "n_col", "row", "col", "frac", "clear", "delta")
 707    out = {k: np.concatenate([p[k] for p in parts]).astype(float) for k in keys}
 708    out["clear"] = out["clear"] > 0
 709    out["upper_lower"] = np.concatenate([p["upper_lower"] for p in parts]).reshape(-1, 2).astype(float)
 710    return out

 713def _pool_checks(results: list[dict], k: int) -> dict[str, np.ndarray] | None:
 714    """All trees' cross-sections when traced again with the depth moved by ``CHECK_MOVES[k]``."""
 715    parts = []
 716    for r in results:
 717        if len(r["checks"]) <= k:
 718            continue
 719        c = r["checks"][k]
 720        s, use = c["sections"], c["closed"]
 721        parts.append({"n_row": s["n_row"][use], "n_col": s["n_col"][use], "clear": s["clear"][use],
 722                      "delta": centre_difference(s["first"][use], s["last"][use], c["d0"][use], c["d1"][use])})
 723    if not parts:
 724        return None
 725    return {key: np.concatenate([p[key] for p in parts]) for key in ("n_row", "n_col", "clear", "delta")}

 728def _row_move_from_checks(remaining: Sequence[tuple[float, float]]) -> float | None:
 729    """The rows moved at which the remaining ``dy`` of the checks would be zero (linear between them)."""
 730    (m1, r1), (m2, r2) = [(float(move[0]), float(rem[0])) for move, rem in zip(CHECK_MOVES, remaining)][:2]
 731    if not (np.isfinite(r1) and np.isfinite(r2)) or r1 == r2:
 732        return None
 733    return m1 + (m2 - m1) * r1 / (r1 - r2)

 747def correction_estimates(results: list[dict]) -> dict:
 748    """The move of the depth that registers it, estimated in each way from these trees.
 749
 750    ``path_*``: the depth moved by each of :data:`TRIAL_MOVES` with the paths
 751    kept, the colour measured on the path, and the move that leaves no fitted
 752    shift (:func:`zero_move`). ``anchored_*``: the same with the colour
 753    measured on the depth run's centre (:func:`anchored_differences`).
 754    ``retraced_*``: the shift left when each tree is traced again with the
 755    depth moved by each of :data:`CHECK_MOVES`, and the rows moved at which
 756    it would be none. ``*_clear``: only cross-sections whose two edges are
 757    both clear.
 758
 759    Args:
 760        results: One :func:`measure_tree` result per tree.
 761
 762    Returns:
 763        ``{key: {"move": [rows, cols] (cols None for the re-traced ones) or
 764        None, "n": cross-sections at no move, ...}}`` for each key of
 765        :data:`ESTIMATES`.
 766    """
 767    moves = list(TRIAL_MOVES)
 768    home = moves.index((0, 0))
 769    out = {}
 770    for anchored in (False, True):
 771        pools = [_pool(results, m, anchored=anchored) for m in range(len(moves))]
 772        for clear in (False, True):
 773            fits = [_fit(p, p["clear"] if clear else None) for p in pools]
 774            zm = zero_move(moves, fits)
 775            key = f"{'anchored' if anchored else 'path'}_{'clear' if clear else 'all'}"
 776            out[key] = {"move": zm["move"] if zm else None, "response": zm["response"] if zm else None,
 777                        "at_no_move": [float(v) for v in fits[home]],
 778                        "n": int(pools[home]["clear"].sum() if clear else len(pools[home]["delta"]))}
 779    for clear in (False, True):
 780        remaining, counts = [], []
 781        for k in range(len(CHECK_MOVES)):
 782            p = _pool_checks(results, k)
 783            if p is None:
 784                remaining.append((float("nan"), float("nan")))
 785                counts.append(0)
 786                continue
 787            remaining.append(_fit(p, p["clear"] if clear else None))
 788            counts.append(int(p["clear"].sum() if clear else len(p["delta"])))
 789        rows_move = _row_move_from_checks(remaining) if len(CHECK_MOVES) >= 2 else None
 790        out[f"retraced_{'clear' if clear else 'all'}"] = {
 791            "move": [rows_move, None] if rows_move is not None else None, "n": counts[0] if counts else 0,
 792            "remaining": [{"move": list(m), "dy": float(r[0]), "dx": float(r[1]), "n": n}
 793                          for m, r, n in zip(CHECK_MOVES, remaining, counts)]}
 794    return out

 835        seed: Random seed of the bootstrap.
 836
 837    Returns:
 838        The report (``report.json``).
 839    """
 840    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
 841    moves = list(TRIAL_MOVES)
 842    home = moves.index((0, 0))
 843    trees = [r["tree"] for r in results]
 844    rows = np.array([r["row"] for r in results])
 845    report: dict = {"trees": trees, "n_trees": len(results)}
 846
 847    # Fitted shift at every move (the usable cross-sections at each), and the move that leaves none.
 848    grid = []
 849    for m in range(len(moves)):
 850        p = _pool(results, m)
 851        dy, dx = _fit(p)
 852        slope = np.abs(p["n_row"]) > SLOPING
 853        up, low = p["upper_lower"][slope].T
 854        grid.append({"move": list(moves[m]), "n": int(len(p["delta"])), "dy": dy, "dx": dx,
 855                     "upper": float(up.mean()) if len(up) else None, "lower": float(low.mean()) if len(low) else None})
 856    loaded = _pool(results, home)
 857    everywhere = [r["closed"].all(0) for r in results]              # usable at every move
 858    fixed = _pool(results, home, everywhere)
 859    fdy, fdx = _fit(fixed)
 860    of_tree = [np.nonzero(fixed["tree"] == t)[0] for t in range(len(results))]
 861    boots = []
 862    for _ in range(n_boot):
 863        sel = np.concatenate([of_tree[t] for t in rng.integers(0, len(results), len(results))])
 864        boots.append(fit_shift(fixed["n_row"][sel], fixed["n_col"][sel], fixed["delta"][sel]))
 865    boots = np.array(boots).reshape(-1, 2)
 866    report["grid"] = grid
 867    report["loaded"] = {"n": int(len(loaded["delta"])), "dy": grid[home]["dy"], "dx": grid[home]["dx"]}
 868    report["fixed_set"] = {"n": int(len(fixed["delta"])), "dy": fdy, "dx": fdx,
 869                           "ci_dy": _ci(boots[:, 0]) if n_boot else None, "ci_dx": _ci(boots[:, 1]) if n_boot else None}
 870    report["estimates"] = correction_estimates(results)
 871    report["zero_move"] = zero_move(moves, [(g["dy"], g["dx"]) for g in grid])
 872    # Equal overshoot at the upper and lower edges, along the rows moved (at the whole column move
 873    # nearest the zero move's): where the mean difference changes sign.
 874    zm = report["zero_move"]
 875    col_move = int(np.clip(np.round(zm["move"][1]), -1, 2)) if zm else 0
 876    line = sorted((g["move"][0], g["upper"] - g["lower"]) for g in grid
 877                  if g["move"][1] == col_move and g["upper"] is not None)
 878    equal = None
 879    for (m0, v0), (m1, v1) in zip(line, line[1:]):
 880        if v0 == 0 or (v0 < 0) != (v1 < 0):
 881            equal = float(m0 + (m1 - m0) * v0 / (v0 - v1)) if v0 != v1 else float(m0)
 882            break
 883    report["edges_equal"] = {"col_move": col_move, "row_move": equal,
 884                             "upper_minus_lower": [[float(m), float(v)] for m, v in line]}
 885    # Across the image: bands and an affine term (at the depth as loaded, fixed set).
 886    report["row_bands"] = [dict(zip(("rows", "n", "dy", "dx"), (list(b), int(s.sum()), *_fit(fixed, s))))
 887                           for b in ROW_BANDS for s in [(fixed["row"] >= b[0]) & (fixed["row"] < b[1])]]
 888    report["col_bands"] = [dict(zip(("cols", "n", "dy", "dx"), (list(b), int(s.sum()), *_fit(fixed, s))))
 889                           for b in COL_BANDS for s in [(fixed["col"] >= b[0]) & (fixed["col"] < b[1])]]
 890    report["affine"] = fit_affine(fixed["n_row"], fixed["n_col"], fixed["row"], fixed["col"], fixed["delta"])
 891    # Per tree (fixed set): the shift, the two halves of the leaders, and the depth along them.
 892    per_tree, near_far = [], []
 893    for t in range(len(results)):
 894        own = fixed["tree"] == t
 895        per_tree.append(_fit(fixed, own) if own.sum() >= 40 else (float("nan"), float("nan")))
 896        near_far.append([_fit(fixed, own & half)[0] if (own & half).sum() >= 20 else float("nan")
 897                         for half in (fixed["frac"] < 0.5, fixed["frac"] >= 0.5)])
 898    per_tree, near_far = np.array(per_tree), np.array(near_far)
 899    depth_values = np.array([r["depth_value"] for r in results])
 900    ok = np.isfinite(per_tree[:, 0]) & np.isfinite(depth_values)
 901    report["per_tree"] = {
 902        "dy": _quartiles(per_tree[:, 0]), "dx": _quartiles(per_tree[:, 1]),
 903        "dy_positive": int((per_tree[:, 0] > 0).sum()),
 904        "split_half": split_half(near_far[:, 0], near_far[:, 1]),
 905        "depth_trend": {"spearman": float(spearmanr(depth_values[ok], per_tree[ok, 0]).statistic)
 906                        if ok.sum() > 5 else None,
 907                        "slope_per_10_levels": float(10 * huber_fit(np.c_[np.ones(ok.sum()), depth_values[ok]],
 908                                                                    per_tree[ok, 0])[1]) if ok.sum() > 5 else None,
 909                        "depth_levels": _quartiles(depth_values)}}
 910    # Mutual information, pooled and per tree, in both seasons.
 911    surfaces = {s: np.stack([r["mi"][s] for r in results]).astype(float) for s in ("winter", "summer")}
 912    report["mi"] = {}
 913    for season, surf in surfaces.items():
 914        peaks = np.array([mi_peak(s[0]) for s in surf])
 915        inside = ~peaks[:, 2].astype(bool)
 916        report["mi"][season] = {**_mi_estimates(surf, rng, n_boot),
 917                                "per_tree": {"dy": _quartiles(peaks[:, 0]), "dx": _quartiles(peaks[:, 1]),
 918                                             "at_edge": int((~inside).sum())}}
 919        # Do trees that the leaders put lower also come out lower by mutual information? The two
 920        # measures share no error source but the images, so agreement means the trees really differ.
 921        both = inside & np.isfinite(per_tree[:, 0])
 922        report["mi"][season]["per_tree"]["vs_leaders"] = split_half(per_tree[both, 0], peaks[both, 0])
 923    # Per orchard row: the fit (fixed set), the move that leaves no shift, and mutual information.
 924    by_row = []
 925    for row in sorted(set(rows.tolist())):
 926        members = np.nonzero(rows == row)[0]
 927        sel = np.isin(fixed["tree"], members)
 928        row_grid = []
 929        for m in range(len(moves)):
 930            p = _pool([results[t] for t in members], m)
 931            row_grid.append(_fit(p))
 932        zm_row = zero_move(moves, row_grid)
 933        estimates = correction_estimates([results[t] for t in members])
 934        dy, dx = _fit(fixed, sel)
 935        # Bootstrap over the row's trees (a row of one tree has no interval).
 936        picks = [rng.choice(members, len(members)) for _ in range(n_boot if len(members) > 1 else 0)]
 937        boot = np.array([fit_shift(*(fixed[k][np.concatenate([of_tree[t] for t in pick])]
 938                                     for k in ("n_row", "n_col", "delta"))) for pick in picks]).reshape(-1, 2)
 939        entry = {"row": int(row), "n_trees": int(len(members)), "n": int(sel.sum()),
 940                 "time": min(results[t]["time"] for t in members), "dy": dy, "dx": dx,
 941                 "ci_dy": _ci(boot[:, 0]) if len(boot) else None, "ci_dx": _ci(boot[:, 1]) if len(boot) else None,
 942                 "zero_move": zm_row["move"] if zm_row else None,
 943                 "estimates": {key: est["move"] for key, est in estimates.items()}}
 944        for season, surf in surfaces.items():
 945            dy, dx, edge = mi_peak(surf[members, 0].sum(0))
 946            boot = np.array([mi_peak(surf[pick, 0].sum(0))[:2] for pick in picks]).reshape(-1, 2)
 947            entry[f"mi_{season}"] = {"dy": dy, "dx": dx, "at_edge": edge,
 948                                     "ci_dy": _ci(boot[:, 0]) if len(boot) else None,
 949                                     "ci_dx": _ci(boot[:, 1]) if len(boot) else None}
 950        by_row.append(entry)
 951    report["by_row"] = by_row
 952    # Rows as replicates: the mean of the rows' estimates, and its standard error.
 953    usable_rows = [r for r in by_row if r["n_trees"] >= MIN_ROW_TREES]
 954    report["across_rows"] = {"n_rows": len(usable_rows), "min_trees": MIN_ROW_TREES}
 955    for key, _ in ESTIMATES:
 956        values = np.array([r["estimates"][key][0] for r in usable_rows
 957                           if r["estimates"][key] is not None and r["estimates"][key][0] is not None], float)
 958        report["across_rows"][key] = ({"mean": float(values.mean()), "se": float(values.std(ddof=1) / np.sqrt(len(values))),
 959                                       "range": [float(values.min()), float(values.max())], "n_rows": int(len(values))}
 960                                      if len(values) >= 2 else None)
 961    report["per_tree_rows"] = [{"tree": results[t]["tree"], "row": int(rows[t]), "time": results[t]["time"],
 962                                "n": int((fixed["tree"] == t).sum()), "dy": per_tree[t, 0], "dx": per_tree[t, 1],
 963                                "dy_near": near_far[t, 0], "dy_far": near_far[t, 1],
 964                                "depth_value": float(depth_values[t]),
 965                                **{f"mi_{s}_{k}": v for s in ("winter", "summer")
 966                                   for k, v in zip(("dy", "dx", "at_edge"), mi_peak(surfaces[s][t, 0]))}}
 967                               for t in range(len(results))]
 968    return report

1072def main(argv: Sequence[str] | None = None) -> int:
1073    """Measure development-row trees; print and save the results.
1074
1075    Args:
1076        argv: Command-line arguments; ``None`` reads ``sys.argv[1:]``.
1077
1078    Returns:
1079        The exit status: 0 on success, 1 if no annotated development tree was found or an output file could
1080        not be written.
1081    """
1082    from concurrent.futures import ProcessPoolExecutor
1083
1084    from treeskel.io import DEPTH_SHIFT, index_dataset
1085
1086    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser(prog="python -m treeskel.measure_registration",
1087                                 description=__doc__.splitlines()[0])
1088    ap.add_argument("data_dirs", nargs="+", help="dataset folder(s)")
1089    ap.add_argument("--clicks", default="annotations/winter_clicks.json")
1090    ap.add_argument("--split", default="splits.json", help="its test rows are never measured")
1091    ap.add_argument("--out", default="outputs/measure_registration")
1092    ap.add_argument("--jobs", type=int, default=os.cpu_count() or 1)
1093    ap.add_argument("--registered", action="store_true",
1094                    help=f"load the depth moved by treeskel.io.DEPTH_SHIFT ({DEPTH_SHIFT}), to check the correction")
1095    ap.add_argument("--boot", type=int, default=N_BOOT, help="bootstrap resamples of the trees (0: none)")
1096    ap.add_argument("--plot", help="also save a chart of the shift by orchard row as this image")
1097    args = ap.parse_args(argv)
1098
1099    # Every annotated development-row tree; the test rows are never measured.
1100    split = json.loads(Path(args.split).read_text())
1101    test_rows = set(split["test_rows"])
1102    clicks = load_clicks(args.clicks)
1103    records = {t.tree_id: t for d in args.data_dirs for t in index_dataset(d).trees}
1104    ids = sorted(t for t, c in clicks.items()
1105                 if t in records and c.complete and not c.skip and records[t].row not in test_rows)
1106    held_out = sorted(t for t in records if records[t].row in test_rows)
1107    if not ids:
1108        print("No annotated development-row trees found; nothing to measure.")
1109        return 1
1110    jobs = [(records[t], clicks[t], args.registered) for t in ids]
1111    state = f"moved by {DEPTH_SHIFT}" if args.registered else "as stored"
1112    print(f"Measuring {len(ids)} development-row trees ({len(held_out)} test-row trees left out), depth {state}...",
1113          flush=True)
1114    t0 = time.time()
1115    step = max(1, min(25, len(jobs) // 10))
1116    results = []
1117    # Each tree's cross-sections in a worker process, with progress about every 10% of the trees.
1118    with ProcessPoolExecutor(max_workers=max(1, min(args.jobs, len(jobs)))) as ex:
1119        for i, r in enumerate(ex.map(measure_tree, jobs, chunksize=2), 1):
1120            results.append(r)
1121            if i % step == 0 or i == len(jobs):
1122                el = time.time() - t0
1123                print(f"  {i}/{len(jobs)} trees, {el:.0f} s elapsed, about {el / i * (len(jobs) - i):.0f} s left",
1124                      flush=True)
1125    # Pool all trees' cross-sections into the shift estimates (the bootstrap resamples whole trees).
1126    print("Pooling (with a bootstrap over trees)...", flush=True)
1127    report = summarise(results, n_boot=args.boot)
1128    report["depth"] = state
1129    report["test_rows_excluded"] = sorted(test_rows)
1130    print_report(report)
1131
1132    # Save the report (NaN written as null) and the per-tree table; a locked file is listed, not fatal.
1133    out = Path(args.out)
1134    out.mkdir(parents=True, exist_ok=True)
1135    failed: list[str] = []
1136    saved = []
1137    for name, write in (("report.json", lambda f: json.dump(_no_nan({k: v for k, v in report.items()
1138                                                                     if k != "per_tree_rows"}), f, indent=2)),
1139                        ("trees.csv", lambda f: _write_trees(f, report["per_tree_rows"]))):
1140        try:
1141            with open(out / name, "w", newline="") as f:
1142                write(f)
1143            saved.append(str(out / name))
1144        except OSError as e:
1145            failed.append(f"{out / name}: {e.strerror or e}")
1146    if args.plot:
1147        plot_rows(report, Path(args.plot), failed)
1148        saved.append(args.plot)
1149    print(f"Saved {', '.join(saved)}")
1150    report_failed(failed)
1151    return 1 if failed else 0

def huber_fit( X: numpy.ndarray, y: numpy.ndarray, k: float = 1.345, iters: int = 40) -> numpy.ndarray: 
365def huber_fit(X: np.ndarray, y: np.ndarray, k: float = 1.345, iters: int = 40) -> np.ndarray:
366    """Robust linear least squares ``y ≈ X @ c`` by Huber's M-estimator.
367
368    Iteratively reweighted: residuals within ``k`` robust standard deviations
369    (1.4826 × the median absolute deviation of the residuals from their
370    median) get full weight, larger ones ``k·σ/|r|``.
371
372    Args:
373        X: ``(n, p)`` design matrix.
374        y: ``(n,)`` values.
375        k: Huber's tuning constant (in robust SDs).
376        iters: Most reweighting rounds.
377
378    Returns:
379        ``c``; zeros if ``y`` is empty.
380    """
381    X, y = np.asarray(X, float), np.asarray(y, float)
382    if len(y) == 0:
383        return np.zeros(X.shape[1] if X.ndim == 2 else 1)
384    w = np.ones(len(y))
385    c = np.zeros(X.shape[1])
386    for _ in range(iters):
387        sw = np.sqrt(w)
388        c, *_ = np.linalg.lstsq(X * sw[:, None], y * sw, rcond=None)
389        r = y - X @ c
390        s = 1.4826 * np.median(np.abs(r - np.median(r))) or 1.0
391        w = np.where(np.abs(r) <= k * s, 1.0, k * s / np.maximum(np.abs(r), 1e-12))
392    return c

513    """Mutual information between the colour image and the depth, for each shift of the depth.
514

516    depth at ``x + (dy, dx)``, so the shift with the most information is where

520

522        rgb: One tree's ``(480, 640, 3)`` uint8 photo, for one season.

533            then exceed ``reach`` by the move.

538    h, w = depth.shape

541    colour = ((q[..., 0] * levels + q[..., 1]) * levels + q[..., 2])[m:h - m, m:w - m]

544    # Rows of each band inside the margin, relative to the compared region.
545    spans = [(max(lo, m) - m, max(min(hi, h - m) - m, max(lo, m) - m)) for lo, hi in bands]

548        a[1] == b[0] for a, b in zip(spans, spans[1:]))
549    size = levels ** 3 * n_depth

551    for i, dy in enumerate(range(-reach, reach + 1)):
552        for j, dx in enumerate(range(-reach, reach + 1)):

def mi_peak( surface: numpy.ndarray, window: int = 3, iters: int = 5) -> tuple[float, float, bool]: 
562def mi_peak(surface: np.ndarray, window: int = PEAK_WINDOW, iters: int = 5) -> tuple[float, float, bool]:
563    """The centre of the peak of mutual information, between pixels.
564
565    Depth reaches past the edges of what it sees (by about 2 px), so every
566    shift that keeps the colour's objects inside the depth's scores about the
567    same: the peak is a plateau, and its highest point is decided by noise.
568    On synthetic scenes whose depth overshoots by 2 px, the highest point
569    missed the true shift by up to 1.5 px (a parabola through it and its
570    neighbours did not help). So a quadratic surface is fitted by least
571    squares to the ``(2·window + 1)²`` values around the highest point, and
572    the window is moved to the vertex and fitted again until it settles;
573    the vertex is the estimate. On the same scenes it was within 0.15 px.
574
575    Args:
576        surface: ``(2·reach + 1, 2·reach + 1)``, one entry of :func:`mi_surface` (or a sum).
577        window: Half-size of the fitted window.
578        iters: Most refits.
579
580    Returns:
581        ``(dy, dx, at_edge)``; ``at_edge`` is True if the window could not be
582        centred on the peak (it lies within ``window`` of the edge of the
583        search, or beyond it) or the fit has no maximum, when the peak may lie
584        further out than was searched. The shift is kept within the search
585        (on all 449 development trees, one row's winter surface extrapolated
586        to 113 px).
587    """
588    size = surface.shape[0]
589    reach = size // 2
590    w = min(window, reach)
591    # Start from the top of the lightly smoothed surface: the highest single value wanders on a plateau.
592    smooth = ndi.gaussian_filter(np.asarray(surface, float), 1.0, mode="nearest")
593    peak = np.array(np.unravel_index(int(np.argmax(smooth)), surface.shape), float)
594    offsets = np.mgrid[-w:w + 1, -w:w + 1].reshape(2, -1).T.astype(float)
595    design = np.c_[np.ones(len(offsets)), offsets, offsets ** 2, offsets[:, 0] * offsets[:, 1]]
596    ok = True
597    for _ in range(iters):
598        centre = np.clip(np.round(peak).astype(int), w, size - 1 - w)
599        z = surface[centre[0] - w:centre[0] + w + 1, centre[1] - w:centre[1] + w + 1].ravel()
600        c, *_ = np.linalg.lstsq(design, z, rcond=None)
601        hess = np.array([[2 * c[3], c[5]], [c[5], 2 * c[4]]])
602        if np.linalg.eigvalsh(hess).max() >= 0:                        # no maximum: keep the highest point
603            ok = False
604            break
605        new = centre + np.linalg.solve(hess, -c[1:3])
606        if np.allclose(new, peak, atol=1e-3):
607            peak = new
608            break
609        peak = new
610    edge = not ok or bool(np.any(np.abs(peak - reach) > reach - w))
611    # A vertex far outside the fitted window means nothing: report at most the edge of the search.
612    peak = np.clip(peak, 0, size - 1)
613    return float(peak[0] - reach), float(peak[1] - reach), edge
