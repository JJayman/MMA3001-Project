# Session summary: 26 September 2026 (session 6)

Fixing how the ground-truth masks join the trunk to the leaders, measured
against the winter depth and checked on all development rows. Continues from
[session 5](2026-09-25-session-5.md).

## 1. The problem

- **Seen by me:** the masks looked like "a big cylinder with two branches
  coming off it". The join between the trunk and the leaders needed to look
  more like a real tree.
- **Cause:** each part's width is one straight taper fitted along its whole
  length. At the fork the trunk ends in a round cap (radius R = 11–22 px),
  and the leaders start at a median of 0.27 R (3–5 px) out of the top of it.
  That leaves a notch, a **shoulder**, on the outer side of each leader.
- The winter depth shows wood in the shoulders: the trunk's edge runs into
  the leader's outer edge.
- Designs were only tried on development-row trees: the 17 tuning trees plus
  R01N01, R01N02, R15N05 and R20N06 (21 trees). The test-row trees in
  `data/sample` were left out.

## 2. How the fork was measured (`treeskel.measure_fork`)

- **Shoulder triangle:** for each leader, the triangle between the fork, the
  trunk's edge level with the fork, and the leader's outer edge 1.5 R or 2 R
  up the leader.
- **Coverage:** the fraction of the triangle that is wood (the depth
  silhouette) or mask. Depth bleeds past thin branches' edges (session 5),
  so the silhouette is also shrunk by 2 px. The true coverage lies between
  the two.
- **Not used to choose the setting:** IoU against depth. It kept rising with
  any widening, because depth bleeds.
- **Independent check:** how far from the fork the depth width along a leader
  takes to fall to the leader's own width (the **width decay**), in trunk
  radii.

## 3. Designs tried (21 trees)

| Design | Result | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Smooth flare: each leader's radius blended from the trunk's at the fork over 1.4 R, both sides | Pixels added in the crotch: 62% on depth (outer sides 87%) | Rejected: fills the crotch |
| The same flare, outer side only | Synthetic fork of known shape: only 41% of added pixels were wood; IoU 0.931 → 0.925 | Rejected: bulges past a straight trunk's edge |
| Round fillet (closing, radius 1.5 R, outer sides) | Synthetic: 100% wood, IoU 0.941; real: 89% on depth | Rejected: only rounds the corner, the round cap still shows |
| Straight fill using the trunk's last R px | Real: 86% on depth, wedges under wide-angle leaders | Rejected: the edge starts below the fork |
| **Straight fill: convex hull of the trunk's end disc and the leader's first 1.4 R, outer side only** | Synthetic: 100% wood, IoU 0.931 → 0.945; real: 94% on depth | **Chosen** (`shoulder_fill`) |

- The chosen fill cannot reach outside that hull, so it never widens a
  straight trunk. The crotch between the leaders is left as drawn.
- The synthetic tree is a wide trunk (31 px) with 9 px leaders, and straight
  chamfers filling the shoulders. It became a test (`wide_fork_rgbd`).
- `shoulder_length = 0` reproduces the old masks exactly.
- The fill adds about 3 ms per tree.
- Figures: `docs/figures/fork_shoulders_before_after.jpg` (six trees: before,
  1.4, 2.0) and `docs/figures/fork_fill_alternatives_synthetic.png`.

## 4. Choosing `shoulder_length` (21 trees)

| Mean shoulder coverage | reach 1.5 R | reach 2 R |
|---|---|---|
| Depth (wood) | 0.980 | 0.966 |
| Depth shrunk 2 px | 0.947 | 0.927 |
| Masks before | 0.861 | 0.782 |
| **Fill 1.4** | **0.987** | **0.885** |
| Fill 2.0 | 1.000 | 0.998 |

- The settings that match depth range from 1.23 to 1.80 (by reach and bleed
  correction).
- The width decay was 1.38 R (leave-one-tree-out 1.35–1.43).
- **Chosen by me: 1.4**, after comparing 1.4 and 2.0 in the figure.
- **No single setting matches both reaches.** The fill's edge is straight
  while the real edge curves: near the fork it covers slightly more than
  depth, further up slightly less.

## 5. Check on all development rows (449 trees, my PC)

| | Coverage 1.5 R | 2 R | Notched (< 80%) 1.5 R | 2 R |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Depth (wood) | 0.965 | 0.950 | 5% | 8% |
| Depth shrunk 2 px | 0.924 | 0.898 | 13% | 19% |
| Masks before | 0.850 | 0.764 | 30% | 66% |
| **Fill 1.4** | **0.984** | **0.872** | **0%** | **23%** |

- **Matching settings:** 1.17–1.72 (mean 1.43).
- **Precision:** 91% of the 22,650 added pixels are on depth. Precision
  around the fork: 0.966 before, 0.965 after. IoU: 0.739 → 0.754.
- **Two claims from the 21 trees did not hold:**
  - the width decay is **1.59 R** (95% CI 1.50–1.68), not 1.38, so 1.4 no
    longer "agrees with the width decay";
  - the added pixels are a little less precise than the rest of the mask
    (91% against 96.6%), not equally precise.
- **Decision (mine): keep 1.4.** It is still near the centre of the direct
  measure, and 1.5–1.6 would reach only 1–3 px further. The docs were
  corrected. Console output: `docs/results/fork_shoulders/all_dev_rows_output.txt`.

## 6. Other findings

- **Constant-width trunks (not changed):** in 15 of 21 trees the trunk is
  drawn with a constant width.
  - Cause: depth cannot separate the trunk from the ground for the first
    5–20% above the root click. The near-zero widths tilt the fit upwards,
    and the "no widening upwards" rule then falls back to a constant.
  - Ignoring widths below half the median makes 6 more trunks taper. But
    trunk precision against depth went 0.976 → 0.974, and the width at the
    fork barely moved (median 15.5 → 15.2 px).
  - Left for a separate decision.
- **R12N09** was flagged by `check_clicks` (fork 2 px above the root). I
  checked it: the leaders start at the base of the trunk. It is noted as
  "splits at ground (checked)", like R07N19 and R17N17.

## 8. AI use in this session (for the reflection)

- **What was mine:**
  - noticing the "cylinder with two branches" look;
  - choosing 1.4 from the figure, and keeping it after the full check;
  - running the 449-tree check and the re-render on my PC;
  - checking R12N09.
- **AI errors and corrections:**
  - **The flare design.** The AI's first design was the smooth flare. It
    looked right in overlays and scored well against depth, but the
    synthetic tree with a known shape showed it bulging past the trunk's
    edge: 41% of its added pixels were wood. Replaced by the straight fill.
  - **The crotch.** The two-sided flare also filled the crotch. This was
    found by splitting the added pixels into crotch and outer sides.
  - **A biased measure.** The AI first compared settings by IoU against
    depth, which rewards any widening. It then built the shoulder-coverage
    measure with a bleed bracket.
  - **A measure that couldn't tell.** A measure of how far out the outer edge
    stays wide could not tell a notch from a flare: the old masks scored
    almost like depth. It was dropped.
  - **The trunk fix.** The AI's fix for constant-width trunks did not improve
    agreement with depth, so it was not adopted.
  - **The first straight fill** included the trunk below the fork and
    overfilled under wide leaders (86% on depth). Using only the trunk's end
    disc fixed it (94%).
  - **Claims from 21 trees** (agreement with the width decay; equal
    precision) failed on 449 trees and were corrected.
  - **Two wrong test expectations** (a degenerate triangle; path length
    against straight-line distance) were fixed after checking the geometry;
    the code was right.
- **Lessons:**
  - A synthetic case with a known answer caught a flaw that the pictures and
    the depth-based numbers missed.
  - A measure that improves with any change (IoU against a reference that
    bleeds) cannot choose a setting.
  - A setting chosen on a small sample needs a check on the full development
    set: one of the supporting numbers moved.
