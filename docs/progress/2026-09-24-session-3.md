## 1. Two versions of the dataset: switched to the N-numbered one

The dataset folder contains two versions:

| | "Dataset" (used until now) | "Dataset (With Summer GT)" (used from now on) |
|---|---|---|
| Tree ids | `R01U01`: U = "unmatched" (photo order) | `R20N03`: N = tree number on the orchard map |

The switch removes the risk of pairing the wrong summer and winter trees. The
row numbers differ between the versions, so the old split was discarded.

## 5. Masks: right shape, shifted position

- The paper's geometry was assumed: the photo's centre 480x480 (columns
  80–559) resized to 256x256. It is implemented as `to_mask_frame` /
  `mask_to_image_frame`. Outside the covered columns, pixels count as
  "unknown", not "no branch".
- **Overlay check on the sample:** the shape (trunk, fork, two leaders, V
  angle) matches, but each mask is **15–45 px left of the visible trunk**,
  varying by tree (clearest: R12N02, `docs/figures/mask_alignment_R12N02.jpg`).
  Stretching the mask to the full width fits worse.
- A depth-based automatic score was tried to pick the geometry. It turned out
  biased (shrinking the mask towards the canopy always scores higher), so it
  was discarded in favour of visual checks where the trunk is visible.
- **Implications:**
  - Masks remain **validation only**.
  - Shape-level comparisons (branch count, V angle, trunk lean, relative
    branch lengths) can be used as they are.
  - Pixel-level metrics (IoU, recall) need a per-tree shift to align each
    mask first. This is the same kind of registration problem as the
    winter→summer alignment, so one method can serve both.
  - Likely cause: the masks were drawn on a slightly different view (for
    example the paper's autumn images). Asked staff.

## 7. AI use in this session (for the reflection)

- **What was mine:**
  - finding the second dataset version and deciding to switch;
  - checking that R03N34's depth is genuine, which reversed the AI's
    exclusion;
  - choosing the sample trees;
  - deciding that masks are for validation only.
- **AI errors, and how they were caught:**
  - The AI said the old split's rows would carry over to the new dataset.
    They don't, because the row numbers differ. It corrected this itself
    once the new row count was known.
  - The AI excluded R03N34 on the evidence of the identical-image check
    alone. I checked the RGB image and showed it was the genuine source.
  - I had judged the masks aligned from thumbnails. The AI's full-resolution
    overlays showed a 15–45 px shift.
  - The AI's automatic depth score for choosing the mask geometry was
    biased, and was dropped after being checked against the images.
- **Lesson:** automatic checks find problems, but deciding what a finding
  means (which copy is genuine, whether an alignment is right) needed
  looking at the actual images.
