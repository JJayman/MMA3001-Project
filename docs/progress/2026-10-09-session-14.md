## 2. What "short leader" means, and session 12's counts (the student's question)

- **The question:** does "short leader" mean a tree whose leader is really short, or a predicted mask whose
  leader is cut short?
- **The rule:** a leader whose skeleton path from the fork is shorter than the stretch the angles are
  fitted over (264 winter px, 158 mask px).
- **Measured with the project's own rule** (my count, and two independent recounts in a multi-agent check):

  | Skeleton | Development leaders short | Test leaders short |
  |---|---|---|
  | Winter traces | 0 of 898 (shortest 324 px; 893 run off the top of the photo) | 0 of 184 |
  | Hand masks | 0 of 896 | 0 of 184 |
  | U-Net (named run) | 37 leaders in 36 of the 447 masks with a fork | 5 leaders in 5 trees |

- **So it always means a predicted leader.** Of the 37 short U-Net leaders, 33 stop inside the photo; 2
  reach the top of the summer view and 2 its side.
- **Session 12's figures mixed two counts:**

  | | Session 12 | By the stated definition |
  |---|---|---|
  | Trees with a short predicted leader | 33 of 444 | **31 of 444 (7%)** |
  | Their share of the V opening's squared error | 47% | **33%** |
  | Ceiling if predicted like the rest | +0.86 | **+0.82** |
  | Bends' share | 20–25% | **30% / 22%** |
  | Median shortfall | 56 mask px | **33** (48.5 over all 37 leaders) |

  - The 33, 47% and +0.86 also counted the 2 masks with no fork (R01N42, R05N05). In them a leader is
    drawn but cut off from the trunk beyond the joining gap: one leader in R01N42, both in R05N05.
  - The 20–25% matches neither count.
  - The 56 px could not be reproduced: session 12 computed these figures in a script that was never
    committed.
- **The 5 stub masks**, left out of the V-opening score, were drawn and looked at:
  - R05N06, R06N28, R14N01: a leader drawn but cut off beyond the gap;
  - R17N11: the second leader is missing;
  - R17N23: the leader finder takes a stub instead of the drawn leader.
- **"13 of 37 have a leftover piece in line"** gives 13 to 19 depending on how "in line" is measured.
- **The final test's "5 of 92, against 33 of 444"** compared two counts. Like for like it is 5 against 31,
  or 6 against 33 with the masks that found no fork.
  - The 5 test trees are not predicted worse than the rest (2.8° against 3.4°).
  - So fewer short leaders explains little of the better test score.
- **Corrected** in the `segment.py` docstrings, the re-run and final-test READMEs and `CLAUDE.md`, with
  "predicted" added wherever "short leader" could be read as the tree.
- **Kept as records:** session 12's notes and `naming_rule.md` keep the original figures.

## 3. The 3D model on the student's PC (`treeskel.model3d`)

**The run** (student's PC): `train` on the 449 development trees, 34 epochs. Then `build` on 4 development
trees, the same 4 from their 5-fold masks (`--mask saved`), and 4 test-row trees.

**The first builds were in-sample.** `train` uses every development tree with its hand mask, so on those
trees the network shows what it learned:

| V opening, photo / plane | Winter | Hand mask | Network trained on the tree | 5-fold mask |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R01N04 | 42.2 / 39.2 | 43.3 / 43.3 | 44.4 / 44.2 | 45.3 / **82.0** |
| R02N08 | 74.7 / 70.3 | 69.2 / 65.0 | 68.3 / 64.0 | 71.1 / 66.9 |
| R06N12 | 53.8 / 52.1 | 57.6 / 57.1 | 55.3 / 54.8 | **39.4** / 39.0 |
| R09N22 | 74.3 / 71.8 | 72.9 / 71.8 | 70.6 / 69.5 | 71.0 / 69.9 |

- The builds from the 5-fold masks reproduce session 13's 3D run exactly.

**A short predicted leader can tip the tree's plane.**
- With one leader short, the trunk and the other leader lie nearly in a line, which hardly fixes a plane.
- R01N04's 5-fold mask stops its left leader after 0.32 m, and the plane leans 58° (seen in the viewer).
- With the 5-fold masks the plane failed on 4 of 442 trees, 3 of them with a short leader (R01N04, R01N40,
  R05N04). That is 10% of the 31 short-leader trees against 1 of the other 411.
- The retrained network's R03N10 (test row) also failed: 87° in the plane, a 50° lean.

**The test-row builds differed from the final test** on three trees:

| V opening in the photo | Winter | Final test | Student's build | Final-test mask |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R12N02 | 48.3 | 49.6 | 50.0 | complete |
| R03N10 | 62.6 | 63.2 | **80.2** | short right leader |
| R16N12 | 71.4 | 70.4 | **61.6** | short right leader |
| R03N32 | 68.8 | no fork | 61.6 | leader 22.4 px from the trunk (gap 19.3) |

**Why: retraining, not the code.**
- **Same prediction path:** one network through `build`'s path and the final test's gave the same input
  images, probabilities and masks, pixel for pixel. The first version of this check was vacuous (see AI
  use).
- **Same training:** on the CPU, the final test's training and `train` gave bit-identical weights (182
  tensors) and identical masks.
- **The cause:** training on a GPU cannot be repeated exactly (cuDNN), so `train` made a second sample of
  the recipe.
- **How much a second sample differs** (session 12's re-run, joined masks, V opening):

  | Trees | n | Median difference | Above 5° | Largest |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Complete in both runs | 393 | 0.4° | 1 tree | 6.1° |
  | Short leader in either run | 49 | 1.3° | 27% | 38.9° |

  - Leaders flip: 35–46% of short-leader masks are complete in the other run.
  - A cut-off leader like R03N32's (19.3–30 px away) is joined in the other run 56% of the time.
  - Both short test trees moving by 9–17° has a chance of about 2%: unlucky, but the pattern fits.
- **For the report:**
  - quote the final test's numbers;
  - instability between trainings is a new property of the short-leader trees.
