## 1. The plan, and a first look (21 trees in the repository)

**The student's plan:**
1. winter first, since its branches are visible;
2. a smooth 3D curve per branch, robust to background readings and depth jumps, joined at the fork;
3. the plane as a preference, not a requirement;
4. summer last, with visible wood as anchors.

The obstacle the student named for step 4: at a hidden branch pixel the depth may be a leaf in front.

**What the depth along the winter traces showed:**
- Smooth structure well above the noise: leaders bowing 5–10 cm over 100–300 px, and trunks behind the
  leaders' plane.
- The trunk's offset is not the ground mixing in: depth profiles across the trunk show clear plateaus
  rising towards the camera.

**A rule for summer readings, the depth ridge:** a reading counts only where both sides of the branch read
at least 4 units (about 5 cm) farther.
- In winter the readings it drops follow the same curves as the ones it keeps, so winter keeps every reading.
- In summer it keeps the trunk below the canopy and almost none of the leaders (0–17% on the preview trees).

## 2. The method (`treeskel.bend3d`)

- **Axis depth:** the depth reads a branch's near surface, so each reading is moved one radius back. Without
  this the trunk would read about 3 cm nearer than the leaders.
- **The curve:** depth = plane + bend `h(s)`, with a Gaussian-process prior (spread τ, length L) whose mean is
  the plane.
  - The three parts share the fork's value, and each may kink there.
  - Each reading counts as `Δs / 7 px` independent ones.
  - Huber weights first, then Tukey's biweight.
  - The posterior SD marks sections as uncertain where it exceeds τ/2.
  - Every point moves along its own ray, so the bent skeleton still projects onto the 2D trace.
- **Block cross-validation:** 40 px blocks are left out with 10 px buffers, and the plane is refitted
  without them. The grid is τ ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16} cm × L ∈ {5, 10, 20, 40} cm, against the plane alone.
- **Alignment-free measures:**
  - the trunk's angle out of the V's plane (the plane through both leader lines);
  - each leader's bow and tilt on its stretch and as a whole;
  - the bent V opening.

**Synthetic checks** (trees rendered as noisy 8-bit depth; `docs/results/bend3d/synthetic_check.py`):
- Flat leaning trees get no bends, and the V is unchanged (−0.03 ± 0.06°).
- Known trunk tilts are recovered to about 1°, and bows to a few millimetres.
- A branch crossing in front gets no weight; a gap stays near the plane and is marked uncertain.
- The prior's marginal SD equals τ.
- The cross-validation finds bends only where there are some.

## 3. The 449-tree run (student's PC, 57 min)

**Rules committed before the run** (58782a6):

2. **Step 1 passes** if the bends beat the plane on more than half of the trees (p < 0.05), also with 30 px
   buffers.
3. **Proposal for the default:** `model3d` bends by default if the summer trunk angle beats the plane with the

## 4. The student's questions after the run

**R01N01:** do the leaders go away from the camera and come back at the top?
- The right leader does exactly that.
- The left leader is an S: first towards the camera, then away, then back.
- Across 449 trees, 77–80% of leaders tilt back over their lower part and less over the whole, at every
  calibration setting tried (zero 262–366), so a wrong zero is not the cause.
- Five posts in the local photos are straight to within 2 cm (lower-minus-whole lean +0.5°, against the
  leaders' +2.4–3°). This suggests the curve is the trees', not a sensor bias with image height, but five
  posts are too few. A 449-tree post check was offered and set aside.

**"The leaders look pretty straight; are the bends scaled wrong?"** No scaling bug. Two causes:
- summer reads almost none of the leaders, so they fell back to the prior's mean, the flat plane;
- the real bends are small, about 4 cm over a 1.4 m leader.

**The fix: the typical winter shape as the prior's mean** (`bend3d --typical`).
- It is the median bend along each part, by distance from the fork, over every development tree's winter
  fit.
- It is applied out of fold, so no tree's own winter reaches its summer model.
- The rule was committed before its run (31bc667): with the U-Net's masks, it must beat the plane-mean bends
  on the trunk angle **and** the leader tilts (p < 0.05).

**The typical-shape run (449 trees): the rule passed.**

| U-Net masks, median \|summer − winter\| | Plane-mean bends | **Typical-shape-mean bends** | Typical shape alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trunk out of the V's plane | 12.0° | **8.5°** (nearer on 78%, p 9e-26) | 7.0° |
| Leader tilts on the stretch | 4.5° | **3.0°** (76%, p 3e-49) | 2.7° |
| V opening | 3.01° | **2.79°** (64%, p 6e-8) | 2.76° |
| Bows | 1.7–1.85 cm | no change | – |

- **The typical leader is an S:** 2–3 cm towards the camera 20 cm up, 1–1.5 cm behind around 0.8 m, back near
  the top. The trunk's root end lies about 3 cm behind the plane.
- **The typical shape alone** (the same for every tree) is nearer still on the angles. Summer's readings
  follow the individual tree (rank 0.38–0.49 against 0.10) but pull the angles low (trunk median 10.9°
  against 14.3°). This was reported, not used: the adopted model shows each tree's own shape.
- **The viewer's "Exaggerate bends" slider** (×1–×5) is display only; the numbers and `.ply` files stay at true
  scale.

## 7. AI use in this session (for the reflection)

**What was mine:**
- the request for bends and the four-step plan, including the leaf obstacle;
- both 449-tree runs on my PC (57 min, then the typical shape), pushing the results;
- the decision to bend by default;
- the question about R01N01;
- noticing that the 3D models' leaders looked straight despite the pattern, and asking whether the bends were
  scaled wrongly, which led to the typical shape;
- regenerating the test report and removing the duplicate tree.

**AI errors, and how they were caught:**
- **The preview's sizes misled again.** The 21-tree preview was labelled "not a result" before the run, but
  the AI still put its 2.2° V widening forward as possibly "cancelling session 13's correction". On 449 trees
  the widening is 1.6° and cancels about two-thirds of the correction; the trunk angle was 14°, not 25°. Row 1
  explains it: 9 of the 21 local trees are from that edge row.
- **`build` did not bend by default at first:** `--bends` and `--flat` shared one setting, and argparse took
  its default from `--bends`. Caught by the new test before the commit.
- **A cross-validation coverage bug:** the last point of a chain whose length was an exact multiple of the
  block was never scored. Caught by a test.
- **A summer tree with no usable readings would have been drawn as fully seen** (no uncertainty). Found on
  self-review before the run.
- **The failed-plane list lumped settings together:** the run printed 12 winter failures where the main
  setting has 4. Fixed in the summary, and the report was remade from the saved rows.
- **Time estimates were half the real time** (30 min estimated, 57 min run).
- **Four `.ply` files were committed by accident;** removed before the PR.
- **A line fitted over a stretch hid the S.** The AI's first answer about the leaders ("away, then back")
  came from the tilts of lines. R01N01's profile, and then the typical shape, showed the first dip towards
  the camera.
- **Figures:**
  - the first palette failed the colour validator (two colours too alike);
  - the first side view in the camera's frame hid the bends;
  - a title used a different "seen" share from the printout.

  All were caught by looking at the figures before committing.
- **Test expectations wrong several times** (a bow's size on the stretch, an argument order, tilt signs,
  vertex counts, a pale share); the code was right.
- **README claims checked against the saved numbers before committing,** with several corrections (the radius
  effect, the whole leaders' tilts, which sessions the small samples misled, a synthetic gap's size).
- **PyTorch could not be installed here in time** (the download was too slow), so the student remade the test
  report.

**Lessons:**
- What a prior's mean is matters most where the data are missing: the flat plane drew straight leaders, the
  typical shape drew the trees' S.
- A straight line over a stretch can hide an S; look at the profiles.
- A small, unrepresentative sample misled for the fifth time. Labelling it was not enough; the AI should not
  repeat its sizes.
- Test the default path of a command, not only its options.
- Fixing the rule before the run kept both decisions honest, and the comparisons the rules did not ask for
  were still reported.
