## 1. Joining nearly-joined masks (`treeskel.segment.join_pieces`)

- **The problem:** the measuring keeps only a mask's largest piece. 101 of the 449 lined-up masks had
  more than one piece, and 14 lost their fork that way.
- **The rule, fixed before scoring:**
  - Pieces are joined to the largest piece when within **19.3 mask px**. That is twice the hand masks'
    median trunk radius, so about a trunk's width.
  - The join is a band between the pieces' centre lines, as wide as the narrower branch.
  - It is applied to every mask.
  - The rule was committed and pushed (4103006) before any joined mask was scored.
- **Result, from the saved masks:**
  - 70 masks changed; masks with no fork went 14 → 2.
  - **V opening +0.72 → +0.75**, better on 8 of 10 measures. The right leader from vertical went
    +0.69 → +0.66.
  - On the 70 changed masks, the V opening moved closer to winter on 27 trees and farther on 16
    (MAE 6.5° → 4.6°, p = 0.013).
- **Sensitivity** (shown, not used to choose): half and double the gap give +0.74 and +0.76. Doubling
  it reaches side branches (right bend +0.53 → +0.42).
- **Where it goes wrong:** a join can attach a side branch (R01N06, R06N12).

## 3. Completing short leaders (the student's ideas, none adopted)

- **The diagnosis (development trees, no winter used in the design):**
  - 33 of 444 trees have a leader shorter than the stretch the angles are fitted over. No hand mask
    does.
  - Those **7% of trees carry 47% of the V opening's squared error**. The ceiling, if they were
    predicted like the rest, is +0.86.
- **The protocol for every idea:**
  - Its settings were fixed without winter and committed before it was scored.
  - It is adopted only if it beats the same network's joined masks tree by tree on the V opening
    (`naming_rule.md` addenda).

| Idea | Settings (fixed without winter) | Result on the V opening |
|---|---|---|
| Grow into the U-Net's less sure pixels (hysteresis); needed a re-run with probabilities saved (18.3 min) | lower threshold chosen by clDice against the lined-up hand masks: 0.1 | every mask grew (604,615 px); MAE 4.105 → 4.141, worse on more trees (p = 0.003) |
| Continue a short leader to a piece on its line, then prune to trunk and leaders | cone 25° (hand masks' 95th-percentile bend 24.8°), within the missing stretch | only 9 masks had such a piece; MAE 4.105 → 4.129, p = 0.21; pruning moved the trunk's centre line and made the angles from the trunk worse (p ≈ 1e-8) |
| Predict the rest of a short leader's line from complete leaders' shapes (`a·g + c·g·bend`) | fitted on complete leaders cut on purpose, per fold (a = −2.78, c = +0.69) | 31 trees changed; MAE 4.100 → 4.102, p = 0.78; on those trees 8.33° → 8.36° |
| Leaves and apples locate the leader (leaf check, 448 trees, student's PC) | descriptive | leaves fill the V (densest 50–60 px inside a leader) and thin outwards; only 1.18× as common near a leader; the leafiest offset is near the leader in 29% of trees against 20% by chance |

- **The re-run repeated the original:** V opening +0.72, joined +0.75. Single measures moved by up to
  0.10 between two runs of the same recipe.
- **Why none helped:**
  - Cutting complete leaders short moves the V opening by 2.7°, about the whole excess error. But real
    short leaders' errors are large (8.3°) and unbiased (+1.1°): the part the network did draw is
    itself unreliable, not just too short.
  - On those trees the U-Net is still much closer to the hand masks than the typical shape is (8.7°
    against 15.7°). So shrinking towards the typical shape would not help either (best weight on the
    U-Net 0.8–1.16).
- **A declared secondary result instead** (the student's request that leaders be "predicted on, not
  cut short"):
  - `draw_completion` draws each short leader on along its predicted line, for the output.
  - The final test reports these masks beside the named model, not instead of it. This was declared
    before the test.

## 4. The final test (`treeskel.final_test`, student's PC)

- **The run:**
  - One network trained on all 449 development trees for 34 epochs (the median of the named run's
    folds), then applied to the 92 test trees.
  - It ran once: the code refuses to run without `model.json` or a second time.
  - 39 s to trace the test trees' winter traces, 2.0 min to train.

| Skill over the development mean shape | U-Net [95% interval] | Hand masks | Development |
|---|---|---|---|
| V opening | **+0.88** [+0.81, +0.92] | +0.91 | +0.75 |
| Left / right bend | +0.66 / +0.62 | +0.67 / +0.65 | +0.60 / +0.53 |
| Left / right leader from the trunk | +0.69 / +0.70 | +0.68 / +0.71 | +0.64 / +0.72 |
| Trunk from vertical | +0.62 | +0.57 | +0.64 |

- **Close to the hand masks:** within their 95% interval on all ten measures. The three rows agree on
  the V opening (+0.86 to +0.89). V opening MAE 3.37° (mean shape 10.4°).
- **Better than development:**
  - The test trees vary as much as the development trees, but the error itself is lower.
  - Possible reasons, which cannot be told apart: training on all 449 trees rather than about 360;
    fewer short leaders (5% against 7%); somewhat easier rows (the hand masks also score higher).
- **Pixels against the test masks as drawn (not lined up):** Dice 0.23, 0.82 after the best shift.
- **The worst tree:** R03N32, the one mask with no fork. Its left leader lies 22.4 px from the trunk,
  just beyond the joining gap.
- **Secondary result (short leaders predicted on):**
  - 5 trees changed; MAE 3.37° → 3.52°, p = 0.14. As in development.
  - It was computed afterwards from the run's saved files (`--secondary-from-saved`), because the pull
    request was merged before the commit that added it reached `main`.
  - The calculation is identical, on the saved masks with the model fixed on development; a test
    checks this.

## 8. AI use in this session (for the reflection)

- **What was mine:**
  - asking to follow the agreed plan;
  - the three runs on my PC (full size, the re-run, the final test) and the leaf check;
  - agreeing the naming rule before the results;
  - noticing that leaders stop short and break;
  - four ideas to complete them, and insisting that the output predict leaders on rather than cut
    them short;
  - choosing the next steps and their order.
- **AI errors, and how they were caught:**
  - **The join's first band was too thin:** the skeleton ends sit near the cut face, where the mask is
    narrow. Caught by a synthetic cut bar before committing.
  - **"No fork" counted two ways:** the re-scoring counted a stub leader as no fork where training runs
    do not. Caught by a synthetic run's test.
  - **"Pruning changes nothing":** the AI said pruning to the trunk and leaders would leave the
    measures as they were. Checked, it moved them by up to 3.9° (the centre lines shift), and the claim
    was corrected before building on it.
  - **The truncation test promised too much:** cutting complete leaders assumed a short leader's
    visible part is right. On real short leaders it is not, so the shape completion that helped on cut
    leaders (2.72° → 1.95°) did nothing on the real ones. The +0.86 ceiling was optimistic for the same
    reason.
  - **The secondary result missed the final test:** the AI pushed a commit to an open pull request
    while I was merging it, without telling me to check the last commit first. Recovered by computing
    it from the saved test files (declared before, identical calculation, a test proves it).
  - **A comparison artefact:** rounded against unrounded values (and winter values stored to 3 decimals
    against 2) made untouched measures look changed. Caught because the completion cannot change the
    bends or the trunk's tilt.
  - **The "middle of the leaves" judged by eye:** the AI's reading from two trees was right in the main,
    but my 448-tree check gave the fuller answer: a weak concentration, and one-sided (inside the V).
  - **Estimates:** the full-size run was estimated at 1.5–2 h; it took 70 min.
  - **Smaller slips, all caught before committing:** a figure subtitle cut off at the edge; test
    expectations after changing a report field's meaning, a fixture, and an exact set of keys.
  - **Environment:** a branch reset was blocked as potentially destructive and replaced by a
    fast-forward; the permission check failed transiently several times.
- **Lessons:**
  - Fix the rule, commit it, then look. Six times this session the order of commits shows that a
    setting or a naming came before its result, and three of my own ideas failed on it.
  - A simulation is only as good as its assumption: cutting good leaders is not the same as having a
    short one.
  - With a result waiting on a merge, say which commit must be in it.
