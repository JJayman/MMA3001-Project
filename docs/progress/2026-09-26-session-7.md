# Session summary: 26 September 2026 (session 7)

Trying to align each tree's winter image with its summer image, so that the
winter masks could label the summer photos. Four approaches failed on the
data, and the direction changed: the next step is to improve the winter
masks, then learn the tree's *shape* from winter, which needs no alignment.
Continues from [session 6](2026-09-26-session-6.md). Details and all numbers
are in `docs/results/align/README.md`.

## 1. The problem

- The winter masks are drawn in the winter image, but a summer model needs
  its labels in the summer image. The camera stood at a different height,
  distance and angle in each season, differently for every tree. Judged by
  eye on the 21 development trees in the repository, the view moves by
  roughly −40 to +90 px sideways and 10–40 px vertically.
- **Visible in both seasons:** the lower trunk, the drip line, and posts
  where there are any. Leaves hide most of the leaders in summer.
- **The trunk base is usually out of frame in winter:** 18 of 21 root clicks
  are in rows 459–477 of 480.

## 2. Automatic matching (`treeskel.align`)

| Approach | Result |
|---|---|

| Orientation correlation (prototype) | Wrong by eye; on R09N12 it placed the trunk on leaves about 100 px too high |
| Hand masks as a bridge | Checked on 2 trees: they fit neither season exactly (R20N06's mask trunk is on the winter position) |

- Loading downloaded pretrained weights is blocked in the AI assistant's
  environment, so the matcher ran on the student's PC. Its matches

- **PyTorch setup on the PC:**

  - Measured for the troubleshooting note: the CPU-only build is 124 MB and

## 3. Clicked point pairs (`treeskel.annotate_pairs`, student's decision)

- **Effort:** the student clicked 60 pairs on 11 trees. It took about 2
  minutes a tree, which would be about 18 hours for all 541.

| | Pairs | Similarity leave-one-out error (median) | Above 10 px |
|---|---|---|---|
| All pairs | 60 | 17.8 px | 80% |
| Away from the photos' top and bottom edges | 33 | 11.8 px | 59% |

- **Why the pairs disagree:**
  - Leader tops where a leader leaves the photo are not the same point in
    both seasons, because the framing differs. The student realised this
    independently.
  - Points under leaves had to be guessed in summer.
  - Featureless stretches of posts fix the sideways position but not the
    height.
- **The homography** was worse than the similarity (28.3 px): with noisy
  points, its extra parameters follow the noise.

**Found by the student:**

- **Camera pose.** Height, pitch, roll and yaw differ between the seasons.
  Measured with a D435-like camera: a pitch difference of 3/5/8/10° leaves a
  similarity 5.5/9.2/14.7/18.5 px off (leave-one-out median), while a
  homography is exact. The homography was added to the comparison and the
  tool.
- **Framing.** A winter photo may start partway up the trunk while the
  summer one shows all of it, and the other way round at the top. Those
  summer pixels get no winter label and are marked "unknown"
  (`<tree>_summer_covered.png`). The tool now shades them.
- **Capture order.** A wooden structure behind row 1 in summer is missing in
  winter. Enlarged, it looks like stacked harvest bins, which stand in the
  orchard at picking time, so it does not settle which season came first.
  Still open.

## 4. Lining up the photos (`treeskel.annotate_overlay`, student's decision)

- **The tool:** the summer photo with the winter photo on top. Drag, scroll
  and keys adjust shift, scale, roll and two perspective tilts; space flips
  between the photos.
- **The transform** (`summer_gt.Overlay`) is built from the camera changes:
  shift, scale, roll, and pitch/yaw as `K R K⁻¹`.
- **Speed:** the first version took about 400 ms per change, which the
  student found laggy. It now takes about 63 ms, thanks to Pillow's compiled
  warp, one blended picture and blitting.
- **Visibility:** the student could not see the summer photo behind the
  winter one. The blend now starts at 30% winter, and an outline view shows
  the summer photo with the winter depth silhouette in cyan. Canny edges were
  tried for this and rejected as too cluttered.
- **Result:**
  - The student aligned R01N01 (143 s) and R01N02 (24 s) and skipped R01N04
    after 165 s.
  - The two alignments agreed with the clicked pairs to within 4–12 px.
  - Their **scale** was 1.15 and 1.12, whereas the pairs gave 0.93 and 0.96.
    Almost nothing seen in both seasons fixes the scale, and a 10% error
    moves the leader tips by 20–30 px.
  - The student could not align the trees reliably.

## 5. Decisions (the student's)

- **Stop aligning winter with summer pixel by pixel.** Automatic matching,
  clicked pairs and lining up by hand all hit the same limit: too little
  rigid structure is visible in both seasons.
- **Next: improve the winter masks.** The student saw masks off the branch
  and of the wrong width. Then **learn the tree's shape from winter**: a model
  predicts the V's geometry (e.g. leader angles relative to the trunk, the V
  opening, curvature) from the summer photo, trained on the winter traces.
  Angles need no alignment.
- **Proposed, not chosen yet:** use the provided hand masks as training
  labels, because they are already roughly in the summer view (on 12 local
  trees their trunks are within about 0–40 px of the summer trunk). The
  winter traces would then be the truth for the shape. This would change the
  decision "hand masks are for validation only".

## 6. Code changes this session

- **`treeskel.align`:**
  - matchers (LightGlue, LoFTR, SIFT) and the least-squares estimators
    written out: the Umeyama similarity, affine by linear least squares, and
    the normalised DLT homography;
  - RANSAC with MSAC scoring and optional validity limits, and
    `consistency_table` with `--consistency`;
  - `warp_mask` and `warp_image`;
  - a CLI that resumes, shows progress, stops after 5 failures in a row and
    explains PyTorch load errors.
- **`treeskel.annotate_pairs`:** the pair click tool, with a live fit,
  leave-one-out labels, a model choice and coverage shading.
- **`treeskel.summer_gt`:**
  - `SeasonPairs` and `Overlay`, with leave-one-out errors and a model
    comparison on the same trees;
  - checks: `interior_pairs`, `pair_distances`, and `mask_displacement` for
    repeatability;
  - it writes the summer masks and the covered areas.
- **`treeskel.annotate_overlay`:** the line-up tool (`fast_warp`, blitting,
  the outline view from `depth_silhouette`).
- **Data:** `data/matches/lightglue.npz`, `annotations/season_pairs.json` (11
  trees) and `annotations/season_overlay.json` (3 trees), all from the student.
- **Docs:** `docs/results/align/` (README and console outputs), plus four
  figures (`align_sift_R09N12.jpg`, `align_lightglue_R01N18.jpg`,
  `align_lightglue_21_dev_trees.jpg`, `align_pairs_R01N18.jpg`).
- **Tests:** 125 → 202, all passing. PRs #26–#33.

## 7. AI use in this session (for the reflection)

- **What was mine:**
  - choosing each approach in turn: the learned matcher first, then clicked
    pairs, then the overlay tool;
  - running LightGlue on my PC, and freeing disk space to fix PyTorch;
  - clicking 11 trees and aligning 3;
  - realising the leader tops are not the same physical point;
  - finding the camera-pose and framing differences, and the structure
    behind row 1;
  - reporting the tool's lag and the hidden summer photo;
  - deciding to stop aligning, and the new direction.
- **AI errors, and how they were caught:**
  - **Blocked weights.** The AI tried to load downloaded pretrained
    weights in its own environment. The safety check blocked it; the files
    were deleted and the matcher ran on my PC.
  - **Disk space.** The first GPU-install instructions did not mention disk
    space. With about 3 GB free, the install was probably incomplete
    (`WinError 193`). The AI then measured the sizes and wrote a
    troubleshooting note.
  - **LoFTR download.** The AI's workaround for the LoFTR download (via the
    browser) did not work.
  - **Scale by eye.** The AI judged by eye that the scale hardly changes
    between the seasons. My overlay (1.12–1.15) and the pairs (0.93–0.96)
    showed the scale is simply not pinned down.
  - **Similarity only.** The first pair tool fitted only a similarity, and
    its protocol did not warn against points at the photo's edge. My
    findings led to adding the homography and the edge filter
    (`interior_pairs`).
  - **Untested speed.** The overlay tool was handed over without its speed
    being measured on a realistic window: about 400 ms per change. It was
    profiled and made about 6 times faster.
  - **Hidden summer photo.** The 50% blend hid the summer photo; fixed with
    a 30% start and the outline view.
  - **Test and code slips.** Three wrong test expectations (a coverage
    assertion, a median that should have been a mean, a NumPy 2 doctest
    repr) and a `git rm --cached` slip, all caught before committing. The
    code was right in each case.
  - **Scratch-only analysis.** The first LightGlue analysis was a scratch
    script; it was moved into the package (`--consistency`) so the numbers
    can be reproduced.
- **Lessons:**
  - Three different methods failing at the same point (automatic matching,
    clicked pairs and lining up by hand) says the limit is in the data:
    across these seasons, too little is visible in both photos.
  - My observations of the actual images (leader tops, camera pose,
    framing) found problems the AI's numbers and tools had missed.
  - An interactive tool needs its speed and visibility tested at a realistic
    size before handing it over.

## 8. Next steps

1. **Improve the winter masks** (new chat). Collect examples where the mask
   is off the branch or the wrong width: tree ids from overlays
   (`python -m treeskel.guided_gt data/raw --overlays`) or from flagging with
   `f` in the overlay tool. Find the causes and fix them, measuring before and
   after. The constant-width trunks (session 6, section 6) may be part of
   this.
2. **Learn the tree's shape from winter (alignment-free):**
   - define shape measures from the winter traces that need no alignment:
     leader angles relative to the trunk, the V opening angle, curvature;
   - a model predicting them from summer RGB-D, with the 5 row-grouped folds;
   - baselines: the mean shape, and the shape of the hand masks (a human's
     guess through the leaves).
3. **Optional later:** train on the hand masks, with the winter traces as
   shape truth (section 5).
4. **Open questions:** which season came first; whether the 8-bit depth
   mapping is the same in both seasons (depth could then give the scale).
