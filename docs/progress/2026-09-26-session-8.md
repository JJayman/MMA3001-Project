# Session summary: 26 September 2026 (session 8)

Improving the winter masks, as planned at the end of session 7. The leaders
had been drawn about half as wide as their bark; they are now drawn at
their measured width with a gentle taper. The shoulder fill is off, and at
the fork the trunk has a flat top with straight joins instead of a round
knob. Two further fixes (trunk widths from colour, re-centring the leaders)
were tried and rejected. They led to finding that the winter depth sits
about 4 px below the colour image, which is the first job for session 9.
Continues from [session 6](2026-09-26-session-6.md) and
[session 7](2026-09-26-session-7.md). Details and all numbers are in
`docs/results/leader_width/`, `docs/results/fork_shoulders/`,
`docs/results/fork_junction/` and `docs/results/depth_registration/`.

## 1. Leader widths

- **The student's observation:** the branches were "slightly too thin",
  and the masks looked odd going "from really thick at the trunk to two
  quite thin leaders". The hand masks change width more naturally, and the
  leaders should narrow slightly along their length, "not much".
- **Diagnosis:**
  - Colour cross-sections across the leaders showed bark 13–17 px wide
    where the mask drew 7.4 px.
  - The widths came from scale-space ridge detection (session 5), which
    responds to the sunlit half of a branch lit from one side. On synthetic
    bark shaded like a matt cylinder it returns 0.55–0.6 of the half-width.
    Its session-5 known-answer test had used bark brighter than the
    background right to its edges.
  - Relative to the trunk (3–5 trunk radii up the leaders, 21 trees): hand
    masks 0.55–0.56, depth 0.55–0.57, masks 0.25–0.26.
- **The new measurement** (`branch_edges`): each cross-section is split into
  background | bark | background by least squares, with a quadratic across
  the bark so that its shaded half counts.
  - On synthetic side-lit branches it was right to within 0.5 px in 47 of
    48 cases.
  - Near the fork a cross-section also takes in the other leader, which made
    synthetic leaders look 1.7–2.4 times too wide. Such cross-sections are
    skipped (`clear_of`): the first usable one is a median 2.2 trunk radii
    from the fork.
- **Depth's overshoot, measured directly:** depth reaches a median +2.0 px
  past clear colour edges (IQR 0 to +4; 115,789 edges on 449 trees). Session
  6 had only bracketed this.

| Leader half-width at least 8 trunk radii from the fork (449 trees) | Median | Minus depth's (median, IQR) |
|---|---|---|
| Depth minus its overshoot | 6.5 px | – |
| **Final masks** | **6.9 px** | **+0.0 px (−0.9 to +1.3)** |
| Before session 8 | 3.2 px | −3.2 px (−4.3 to −2.4) |

## 2. The taper along the leaders

- A free straight taper per leader is unreliable: 50% of leaders came out
  widening upwards on the 21 local trees, 28% on all 449.
- So all leaders share one shape: `r(s) = c · (1 + w · exp(−s / (L · R)))`,
  with `c` each leader's own width and `R` the trunk's radius at the fork.
- **Chosen on 21 trees: w = 0.35, L = 4. On all 449 (student's PC): L = 12,
  at the edge of the grid.** The grid was extended (w up to 1.0, L up to 48
  trunk radii), and the row-grouped folds of `splits.json` were added.

| Shared widening (449 trees) | w | L (trunk radii) | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best, and the default** | **0.35** | **12** | **2.4573** |
| First default (from 21 trees) | 0.35 | 4 | 2.4661 |
| Constant width | 0 | – | 2.5114 |

- **Leave-one-tree-out** picked 0.30–0.35 and 12, better than a constant
  width on 297 of 449 held-out trees. **Row-grouped folds** picked 0.30–0.35
  and 8–12, better on 5 of 5 folds.
- **The leaders now narrow gradually**, from 0.52 of the trunk radius at 1
  trunk radius from the fork to 0.42 at 15. The cross-sections themselves go
  from 0.50 at 2 to 0.39 at 15.
- **Three statements made from the 21 trees did not hold on 449:**
  - the taper (median change 0% → −16% on all trees);
  - the length (3–5 → 12);
  - "weak evidence" (the shape beats a constant width on 66% of trees).

  17 of the 21 are the tuning trees, picked in session 4 as problem trees.

## 3. The shoulder fill (student's decision: off)

- On 449 trees, the masks without any fill cover 0.914 and 0.879 of the

## 4. The fork junction

- **The student's observation:** where the trunk meets the leaders the mask
  "kind of bulges and just looks like a circle". The student compared R01N17
  and R01N18 with the hand masks and asked for a fix "not with the fill".
- **Cause:** drawn as discs, the trunk ended in a half disc of its own radius
  at the fork. The same happened at the root of a short trunk.
- **Fix** (`end_cut`, `fork_junction`):
  - the trunk is cut flat at the fork;
  - on each leader's outer side, a straight edge joins the trunk's edge to
    the leader's edge 1 trunk radius up the leader (`junction_reach = 1.0`);
  - the crotch is the leaders' own V;
  - the root is cut flat too, where its round end would lie wholly inside
    the image (`flat_root`, about 1 tree in 10). Most root clicks are a
    median 4 px above the bottom edge, where the trunk leaves the frame, so
    the round end is kept there.
- **The reach.** The AI first proposed 1.5, from the session-6 width decay
  (1.59 trunk radii). Measured, 1.5 over-filled the shoulder, as the 1.4
  fill had. The decay describes the whole junction, crotch included, not the
  outer join. 1.0 matches depth:

| Junction reach at which the joins cover the shoulder as much as | 21 trees | 449 trees |
|---|---|---|
| depth, at 1.5 / 2 trunk radii | 0.98 / 1.30 | 1.04 / 1.23 |
| depth minus its overshoot | below 0.5 / 0.51 | 0.62 / 0.59 |

- **By depth the change is neutral:** precision around the fork 0.941 →
  0.942, IoU 0.833 → 0.831 (449 trees).
- **The crotch claim did not hold.** On 21 trees the V (0.86 trunk radii
  above the fork) looked closer to depth's crotch (0.77) than the round top
  (1.00). On 449, depth's crotch is at 1.07 (0.95 minus its overshoot), and
  the two are equally far from it (±0.06).
- **Known answer:** on a synthetic fork, the V's bottom is within 2 px of
  the true 10 px, where the round top put it at 16 px.
- **The student's check** after re-rendering all masks: R01N17 and R01N18
  "look a lot better now".

## 5. Tried and rejected: trunk widths from colour, re-centring the leaders

- **Trunk width from colour.** On the 21 trees, colour and the drawn trunk
  agree on the median tree, but each cue fails on some trees:
  - colour catches only the sunlit stripe of R01N18's trunk, and takes in a
    white object beside R09N34's;
  - depth takes in a dark object beside R01N05's trunk (probably a tree
    guard), and weeds or the ground elsewhere.

  No cue is reliably better, so the trunk was left as it is.
- **Re-centring the leaders on the colour bark centre.** The "2.5 px off the
  bark's centre" of the width check is mostly noise in single colour
  cross-sections:
  - colour and depth disagree about which side the path is off
    (correlation +0.03);
  - re-centring moved paths away from the depth centre on 28 of 42 leaders;
  - it pulled R07N26's leader onto a dark band beside it, probably a post
    behind the leader.

## 6. Found: the winter depth sits about 4 px below the colour image

- **The fit.** Across 9,988 leader cross-sections, depth's centre minus the
  bark's colour centre fits a fixed image shift: **3.2 px down and 0.6 px
  left**.
- **It is the same everywhere:** 3.2 px in the upper and lower halves, 2.9
  and 2.7 px in the left and right halves, and down on 19 of 21 trees.
- **It shows directly in the edges.** On sloping leaders, depth reaches 0 px
  past the bark's upper edge and 4 px past its lower edge.
- **It is not the lighting.** The sun comes from the right. With a
  lighting term in the fit, the shift stays 3.1 px down.
- **Moving the depth up about 3.8 rows removes it.** Both edges then show the
  2 px overshoot. The traced paths, which the cost map pulls towards
  depth's centre, stop sitting off the bark on sloping leaders
  (−1.32 → +0.17 px × the normal's row component). The widths barely change.
- **Summer is not checked yet:** the check needs traced branches.
- **Nothing is changed yet.** The fork measurements compared the masks with
  a shifted silhouette, so the junction reach and the crotch comparison
  need re-checking after the correction.

## 9. AI use in this session (for the reflection)

- **What was mine:**
  - seeing that the leaders were too thin and the trunk-to-leader change
    looked wrong, pointing to the hand masks, and expecting a slight
    narrowing;
  - running the checks on all 449 trees on my PC (`measure_width` three
    times, `measure_fork` twice) and re-rendering all masks;
  - turning the fill off;
  - asking for a fork fix without the fill, and judging it on R01N17 and
    R01N18;
  - choosing to wrap up before the registration fix.
- **AI errors, and how they were caught:**
  - **Scale-space widths.** The AI's session-5 width method measured only
    the sunlit half of each leader, because its known-answer test used
    unrealistic bark. I saw the thin leaders; cross-sections and a
    synthetic shaded cylinder confirmed it.
  - **Claims from 21 trees.** Four claims made from the 21 local trees
    failed on all 449: the taper, the widening length, "weak evidence", and
    the crotch. The AI knew the sample was biased (17 problem trees) and
    still stated them as findings. My full runs caught them.
  - **The first reach.** The AI argued for a reach of 1.5 from a
    measurement that does not measure it (the width decay). Its own
    measurement corrected this to 1.0 before the PR.
  - **The first junction** also filled part of the crotch (1–61 px per
    tree). Found by measuring the crotch's height against the reach.
  - **The first widening estimate** partly counted the other leader. A
    synthetic tree with constant-width leaders exposed it, which led to the
    neighbour rule.
  - **The "before" masks** in `measure_width` silently picked up the new
    default (no fill). Caught, fixed, checked pixel for pixel against the
    session-7 code, and now guarded by a test.
  - **The "2.5 px off-centre" paths** were reported as a path error; it was
    mostly noise. The two fixes the AI proposed for item 1 (trunk from
    colour, re-centring) both failed validation.
  - **Registration never checked.** For eight sessions the AI's methods
    combined depth and colour without checking that the two images line up.
    The shift surfaced only when two sensors disagreed about a centre.
  - **Smaller slips:**
    - a README table copied by hand (now generated from `report.json`);
    - a test that relied on one random seed;
    - a vacuous test assertion, caught before committing;
    - a script that treated text fields as numbers;
    - an edge-correlation check that could not find the shift even on
      winter (not used).
- **Lessons:**
  - Check that two sensors are registered before combining them.
  - The 21 local trees are unrepresentative: a claim needs the 449-tree
    check before it is stated.
  - A check against depth is not independent of a method that used depth:
    the tracing follows depth's centre.
  - Both mask changes started with my looking at the masks. The numbers
    then found the causes.
