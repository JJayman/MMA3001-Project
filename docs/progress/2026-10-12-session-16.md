## 1. The plan (the student's)

**Two changes, each aimed at the short predicted leaders and their instability between trainings:**
- **The centre-line loss:** it rewards complete leaders, not only pixel overlap. Its risk is rewarding wrong

- **An ensemble of three trainings:** it averages away a leader one training misses. Its risks are the
  computation and blurring uncertain branches.

- **Stubs counted as the mean shape:** a mask with a fork but no V opening was otherwise left out, which
  lets a variant drop its worst trees.
- **A side-branch measure.**
- **The members' spread as a warning.**
- **A second, independent ensemble:** the only way to see an ensemble's own stability.

## 2. Before any run

**The loss** (`segment --cldice`): `BCE + 0.5 Dice + 0.5 clDice`.
- This is Shit et al.'s combination at the default of their public code (checked in their repository),
  with the named recipe's cross-entropy kept.
- **25 soft-skeleton erosions:** a branch whose distance transform peaks at `d` needs `d − 1` erosions
  (tested on synthetic bars and discs). The widest local hand mask peaks at 19 px under the augmentation's
  largest zoom. The authors' 10 erosions would have left the trunk out of the loss.
- **The named recipe is untouched:** a CPU training with the default settings gave the same 182 weight
  tensors as `main`'s code, bit for bit. So session 12's re-run counts as seed 0.
- **The held-out loss:** with clDice it took 9.4 s per epoch on a CPU, about 45 extra minutes per run. It is
  now computed on the GPU, for the centre-line recipe only.

**How much more clDice charges than Dice** (`loss_check.py`, 21 local development hand masks, left leader
cut on purpose):

| Cut | clDice ÷ Dice |
|---|---|
| A leader stopping halfway | 1.63× |
| An 8 px gap | 1.21× |

At α = 0.5 that is 1.32× and 1.11× the Dice term alone. **So the loss is a moderate re-weighting, not a
connectivity loss.** This was written down as the expectation before any run.

**The structure measures** (`treeskel.seg_compare`):
- **Short leaders:** session 14's count is reproduced (36 trees, 37 leaders of the named run's 447 masks
  with a fork).
- **Side branches:** stretches of centre line reaching more than a trunk radius (9.65 px) beyond the
  trunk's and leaders' outline. On the local hand masks no stretch reaches past 2.2 px; on the named run's
  masks 95% stay within 2.8 px.
- **Stray pieces, extra area,** and clDice against the hand masks (coverage).

**The rule** (`rule.md`, committed in aecaed5). A change is adopted if:
- its V-opening MAE is lower, Wilcoxon p < 0.05, each tree averaged over a recipe's three runs; **and**
- it causes no significant increase in side branches or incomplete masks.

**The student's confirmation** (0077892, before the first run):
1. the rule: yes;
2. an adopted change reported once on the test rows: yes;
3. the second ensemble: yes.

## 4. The rule's verdict, and the null check

**As fixed:**

| Change | V opening MAE | p | Guards | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Centre-line loss | 4.375° → 4.140° | 0.009 | held | **passed** |
| Ensembling | 4.375° → 4.293° | 0.002 | held | **passed** |

The rule therefore named the centre-line ensemble.

**The null check** (pre-declared): with seeds 3–5 there are seven trainings of the named recipe, so the
rule's own test can be applied to samples of one recipe (`checks_after_runs.py`).

| Same recipe, compared with the rule's test | Called different (p < 0.05) | Expected by chance |
|---|---|---|
| One training against another (21 pairs) | 5 | about 1 |
| Three trainings against the other three (10 splits) | 3 | about 0.5 |

- One of those splits is the rule's own baseline triple against the other triple (p = 0.027).
- **Why:** a training is better or worse on many trees at once, and a tree-by-tree test counts the trees as
  independent. Averaging three trainings does not remove that.

**What survives:**
- **Centre-line loss:**
  - V-opening gain not established at the level of trainings (permutation p 0.11);
  - against the other triple, 4.206° → 4.140°, p 0.42;
  - coverage of the labelled centre lines rises from 0.866 to 0.883 (all 3 runs above all 7, p 0.008);
  - incomplete masks per training fall from 47.6 to 38.3 (p 0.09).
- **Ensembling:**
  - each ensemble beats its own members (where training shifts cancel) on all three ensembles, by
    0.06–0.14° (p ≤ 0.006);
  - two independent ensembles are a median 0.36° apart, against 0.67° for single trainings (1.03° against
    1.94° where a mask is incomplete);
  - the members' spread flags bad trees for the named recipe (Spearman +0.14 and +0.15; 9° MAE on the 10%
    most spread, against 3.6–3.8°), but not for the centre-line ensemble (+0.07, p 0.12).
- **Figure:** `docs/figures/segment_variants.png`.

## 5. The student's choice, and the test rows

`docs/results/final_test_ensemble/model.json` (390becf) before the run:
- the final test's recipe and 34 epochs;
- seeds 0–2;
- the probabilities averaged.

**The run** (5.8 min to train), every network on the same 92 trees (`like_for_like.py`):

| V opening, test rows | MAE | Skill |
|---|---|---|
| The final test's network (the headline) | 3.37° | +0.88 |
| Seeds 0 / 1 / 2, each alone | 3.63° / 3.88° / 3.64° | +0.85 / +0.81 / +0.86 |
| Their ensemble | 3.78° | +0.81 |

- **No gain:** the ensemble is no better than its own networks (p 0.65), nor than the final test's network
  (p 0.68).
- **Where it lost:** on R03N10 and R16N12, session 14's short-leader trees, two of the three networks failed,
  and the average followed them. This is the "averaging can lose a leader" risk the student named.
- **Where it helped:** R03N32, the final test's one mask with no fork.
- **What it means:**
  - the final test stays the headline;
  - a network of this recipe should be expected to score about +0.81 to +0.88 on these rows;
  - the final test's network was the best of four trainings.

## 9. AI use in this session (for the reflection)

**What was mine:**
- **The plan:** the two changes and their limits, the evaluation's constraints, and the label audit.
- **Before the runs:** answering the rule's three questions.
- **On my PC:** the eight trainings, three ensembles and the comparison; then the one test-row run.
- **Option B:** choosing it over the rule's candidate once the null check had shown the test too lenient.
- **The audit:** saying I wasn't sure what it asked, then judging the 40 trees again.

**AI errors, and how they were caught:**
- **The rule's test.** The AI designed the rule around a tree-by-tree Wilcoxon test, as earlier rules used.
  It took comfort from a single same-recipe pair (p = 0.93). The pre-declared null check, with the second
  ensemble's seeds, showed the test calling the same recipe different in 3 of 10 triple splits, including
  the rule's own baseline. The rule passed a change the evidence did not support.
- **A comparison that dropped the failures.** The test-row run's member comparison left out stubs, unlike
  the AI's own development rule. It dropped exactly the two trees where the ensemble failed, and printed a
  gain (3.51° → 3.34°) where like for like there is none (3.72° → 3.78°). Caught because the printed MAE
  (3.34° on 90 trees) did not match the ensemble's own (3.78° on 92). Recomputed from the saved files; code
  fixed; a test added.
- **Instructions without an example.** The audit was first a CSV to fill in, with definitions but no picture
  of "off". I marked everything `on` without being sure. The AI then built the key-press window and a guide
  picture.
- **Claims checked before committing:**
  - "networks lost the right leader": only their V openings and forks were known;
  - run times (18.3 and 20.4 min, not "18–20");
  - the leaf check, which changed no masks.
- **Caught by tests or measurement before any run:**
  - the audit's allocation could give a row lifted to its minimum a second tree before larger rows had
    theirs (fixed, and the sample redrawn before anyone looked);
  - the held-out clDice loss would have cost about 45 minutes per run on the CPU;
  - stubs would have let a variant drop its worst trees;
  - a `model.json` key clashed with an existing one.
- **Smaller slips, caught before committing:**
  - wrong test expectations (a fixture's size, a run name with its size, a leader count, a member score
    over two trees), where the code was right;
  - a test helper loading 449 trees for each test (7.5 min, then 21 s);
  - a docs annotation warning;
  - a figure label on top of a title, and overlapping dots.
- **Environment:** PyTorch's own index is blocked here, so PyTorch and kornia were installed from PyPI, and
  every test ran (0 skipped).

**Lessons:**
- **A test must be checked on samples that should not differ.** Pre-declaring the null check is what caught
  the rule's flaw; one reassuring pair was not enough.
- **Compare like with like:** every network on the same trees, its failures included.
- **A gain of 0.1° on 449 development trees** does not survive 92 test trees when retraining alone moves the
  test score by 0.5°. The spread between trainings is the yardstick.
- **Show what a judgement looks like before asking for one.**
