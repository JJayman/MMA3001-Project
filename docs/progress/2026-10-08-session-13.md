## 1. The automatic winter baseline against the guided ground truth (`treeskel.score_baseline`)

- **What was compared:** the session-4 automatic method and the traces through the student's clicks, on
  the same winter photos (no alignment needed).
- **Scores, fixed before the run:**
  - *found*: how much of the clicked branches the baseline passes through;
  - *on the branch*: how much of its own lines lie on them;
  - their F1, with a tree **traced** at F1 ≥ 0.9;
  - fork distance and mask overlap;
  - the ten shape measures, scored exactly as the U-Net's.
- **Check:** the traces recomputed here reproduce the committed winter measures (largest difference
  0.006°).

| 449 trees | F1, median | Traced (≥ 0.9) | Fork distance |
|---|---|---|---|
| All | 0.86 | **43%** | 11 px |
| Passed its quality check (340) | 0.91 | 57% | 9 px |
| Flagged (109) | 0.52 | 0% | 79 px |

- **The quality check catches failures, but not all of them.** All flagged trees are wrong, but so are
  147 of the 340 it passes. It notices a leader that stops short, not one that climbs the wrong branch.
- **The student's session-4 verdicts agree with the scores:**
  - the 38 trees judged wrong by eye: median F1 0.67, 2 traced;
  - the other 72 trees of those rows: 0.93, 58% traced (p = 5e-10).
- **Shape, the V opening:**
  - Within 5° of winter on 70% of trees, median error 1.9°: when the baseline works it is the most
    precise method, since it sees the bare tree.
  - More than 20° off on 14% of trees, against the U-Net's 1.4%.
  - Skill −1.97 against the U-Net's +0.75: the summer U-Net, looking through leaves, is more reliable
    than a hand-written rule looking at the bare tree.
- **Lines through the clicks alone** give the trunk's tilt (+0.93) but no bends: tracing was needed.
- Figure: `docs/figures/winter_baseline_examples.jpg`.

## 2. Run time and memory (`treeskel.benchmark`)

- **Measured on the student's PC** (GTX 1080 Ti, 8-thread CPU), the same way for every method.
- **Networks get random weights and random photos**: time and memory do not depend on them.

| Method | V opening skill | Parameters | Predict a photo, GPU / CPU | Train one network |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ridge on ResNet-18 features | +0.10 | 30,730 (+11.2 M fixed) | 5.9 / 51 ms | 71 s for 5 folds (CPU) |
| Angle CNN 240x320 / 480x640 | +0.16 / +0.18 | 11.2 M | 6.1 / 53 ms; 7.7 / 181 ms | 0.7 / 2.0 min |
| **U-Net 256 (named)** | **+0.75** | 14.3 M | **9.7 / 116 ms** | **2.0 min** |
| U-Net 480x640 | +0.74 | 14.3 M | 18.7 / 463 ms | 5.9 min (3.7 GB) |

- **Check:** the estimate for one U-Net (2.00 min) matches the final test's recorded 2.0 min.
- **The named U-Net's accuracy does not come from more computation.** The full-size one costs 3× to
  train and 4× the memory for no gain.
- **It could run on a robot without a GPU:** 0.13 s per photo with the joining and measuring.
- **Ground truths:**
  - the baseline takes 0.16 s per tree;
  - the guided tracing takes 1.2 s plus about 15 s of clicking: the person is the cost.

## 3. The depth map (`treeskel.depth3d`)

- **The map:** distance = scale × (zero − value).
- **Only the zero matters for angles;** the scale cancels, and sizes need both.
- **How the zero was measured:** the flat ground behind each tree only comes out flat for the right
  zero. Ground blocks were fitted for zeros from 190 to 5000.
- **Results (449 trees):**
  - Linear in distance, not in disparity, on **98%** of trees.
  - Zero **300** [quartiles 262, 366]; pooled 325. Session 2's 255 lies below the quartiles.
  - Rows agree (medians 242–344), except row 1, the orchard's edge row.
- **The scale:** nothing in the photos fixes it, so it comes from the documented 1.8–2.2 m from camera
  to canopy (Chen et al.).
- **Not usable for the scale:**
  - The neighbouring trunks mix with posts in the depth.
  - Their gaps (130–300 px) do not clearly match session 2's "0.6 m apart", which was never checked.

## 4. The trees in 3D (`treeskel.tree3d`)

- **Settings, committed before the run** (337b33d):
  - zero 300, focal length 616 px;
  - the median fork at 2.0 m (0.01197 m per unit);
  - every measure also at zeros 262, 366 and 255, and focal lengths 585 and 647.
- **Per tree:**
  - depth read along the trace;
  - a robust plane fitted to it (total least squares, Huber weights);
  - the skeleton moved along its rays onto the plane.

| Winter, 449 trees | Median [quartiles] | Across the calibration range |
|---|---|---|
| Depth from the tree's plane | **1.7 cm** [1.4, 2.2] | – |
| V opening, plane / photo | 51.1° / 53.1° | – |
| **3D correction** (plane − photo) | **−2.3°** [−3.1, −1.6]; negative on 97% of trees | −1.5 to −3.4°, always negative |
| Lean towards the camera | 9.2° | 6.4–12.8° |
| Lean from the vertical (ground) | −12.9° | **−3.1 to −23.8°**: not determined |
| Trunk diameter at the fork | 10.7 cm (about 9.4 without depth's edge overshoot) | ±10% with the distance |

- **The trees are flat:** the "2D" trellis is 2D to within a couple of centimetres.
- **The 2D V is about 2° too wide**, on almost every tree.
  - The lean explains it: a synthetic tree with the median lean and turn gives −2.1°, and the same tree
    facing the camera 0°.
  - Apart from 6 failed planes, the correction varies by only ±1.1°.
  - So the project's 2D results stand.
- **Summer against winter, tree by tree:**
  - The V opening agrees better in the plane than in the photo: closer on 61% of trees with the hand
    masks (p = 2e-6) and 58% with the U-Net (p = 4e-4). The in-plane V does not depend on where the
    camera stood, and the camera moved between the seasons.
  - The correction repeats in part (rank correlation 0.52 / 0.48).
  - The camera-relative lean and turn hardly repeat (0.39 / 0.10): the camera's pitch and yaw changed.
- **Failed planes:** 6 winter trees (1%) have planes turned more than 20°; R08N22 has depth on only 11% of
  its skeleton. They are listed, not removed.
- Figure: `docs/figures/tree3d.png`.

## 5. A summer photo as a 3D model (`treeskel.model3d`, the student's request)

- **What it does:** one summer RGB-D photo in, a 3D model out:
  - the named U-Net's branch mask (pieces joined);
  - its skeleton and widths;
  - the summer depth placing the tree's plane;
  - tubes in metres for the branches, and the summer depth near the plane as coloured points (the leaves).
- **Output:**
  - A self-contained HTML viewer (WebGL 2, no libraries, no internet): drag to turn, scroll to zoom,
    fade or hide the leaves; a panel with the 3D measures.
  - Binary `.ply` files for Blender, MeshLab or 3D Viewer.
- **Commands:**
  - `train` saves the named U-Net once, with the final test's recipe, to the git-ignored `models/`
    (about 2 min on the GPU). **Not yet run on the student's PC.**
  - `build` takes a dataset tree or any photo with its depth.
  - For development trees, `--mask saved` (the 5-fold run's masks) or `--mask hand` need no weights.
- **Checks:**
  - The example (`docs/results/model3d/R15N05.html`) gives the 3D run's numbers for that tree.
  - The tubes are closed surfaces.
  - The PLY files read back exactly.
  - The page was opened and turned in headless Chromium without errors.
- Figure: `docs/figures/model3d_R15N05.jpg` (four views).
- **Limits:**
  - Sizes assume 2 m to the fork.
  - A short leader stays short.
  - The trunk is as wide as the hand masks drew trunks.
  - All branches lie on one plane.

## 8. AI use in this session (for the reflection)

- **What was mine:**
  - asking to do the agreed items, and to keep going;
  - running all four measurements on my PC and pushing the results;
  - asking for the results in plain words for the report;
  - asking for a tool that turns a summer photo into a 3D model I can open and turn.
- **AI errors, and how they were caught:**
  - **Pearson's r swamped by outliers.** The first 3D summary said the per-tree correction does not
    repeat between seasons (r = 0.04), and the first figure's title said so. The AI looked at the figure
    before committing: the points followed the diagonal. Six failed planes (corrections of ±80°) had
    swamped r; the rank correlation is 0.52, and with failures left aside summer agrees *better* in 3D.
    Summaries now use medians and ranks, and a test checks that one failed fit cannot swamp them.
  - **A biased object filter.** The first ground selection used "nearer than the row's median" (from
    `winter_gt`), which also cuts away real ground when the camera rolls. A synthetic ground with a known
    zero gave 233 instead of 255. Replaced by a robust surface fit that assumes no zero.
  - **A pooled estimate that returned nothing:** it dropped every tree with any ruled-out zero. Caught by
    a test.
  - **Claims checked against the numbers before committing**, four of them wrong:
    - "significantly worse than the U-Net": true on the trunk-relative measures, the opposite on the
      angles from the vertical;
    - "2 hours of clicking for 541 trees": session 5 recorded 1 h 50 min for 449;
    - "the same 2 minutes as the angle CNN": that is the full-size one;
    - "8 photos a second": 7 with the joining.
  - **A geometry estimate by formula:** the AI expected the lean to explain about 0.5° of the
    correction; a synthetic tree with the median lean gave 2.1°, the measured size, because of
    perspective. Only the checked statement went into the README.
  - **Judging by eye from thumbnails:** the AI read the neighbouring trunks as 270–320 px apart; measured
    at full size they mixed with posts, and they were not used (session 1's lesson again).
  - **Test fixtures and expectations** (the code was right):
    - a synthetic trunk farther than the ground in front of it;
    - synthetic trees running off the photo;
    - NumPy adding in place into a temporary, which halved an expected memory peak;
    - one vacuous assertion, removed;
    - rounding in saved files (the reports are now made from the saved rows, so they reproduce exactly).
  - **Environment:**
    - pytest and PyTorch were missing here: installed, PyTorch from PyPI because download.pytorch.org is
      blocked;
    - the 10 network tests now run here too.
- **Lessons:**
  - A summary statistic can contradict its own plot; looking at the plot caught it.
  - Test every new estimator on a synthetic case with a known answer first: two of this session's bugs
    surfaced only there.
  - Check every sentence of a write-up against the saved numbers before committing it.
