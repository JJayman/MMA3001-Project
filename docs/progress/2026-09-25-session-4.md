# Session summary: 25 September 2026 (session 4)

Winter ground truth: an automatic method, its failures on the full dataset,
and the switch to click-guided tracing. Continues from
[session 3](2026-09-24-session-3.md).

## 1. Terminology

- **Trunk:** the single stem from the ground up to the fork.
- **Fork:** where the trunk splits.
- **Leaders:** the two main arms of the 2D open-V (Tatura trellis) above the
  fork, one on each side in the image.
- **Branches:** anything smaller growing off the leaders.

The hand masks show the trunk plus two leaders, so the ground truth uses
exactly two leaders, even for the few trees that have a third main branch
(e.g. R01N09, R01N14, R09N04).

## 2. Automatic method (`treeskel.winter_gt`), now the baseline

Winter depth only:
1. Foreground: above the ground line, anything in range; in ground rows,
   pixels nearer than the row median.
2. Tree selection: the large, low-reaching region centred at its base.
3. Skeleton, with gaps bridged only **upwards along the branch direction**.
   Bridging in any direction followed the irrigation wire into
   neighbouring trees.
4. Leaders: one per side, scored by **rise minus a wandering penalty**.
   Choosing by longest path failed on 3 of the 8 sample trees.
5. Quality check (QC): flag the tree if a leader rises less than 30% of the
   root height.

**v2 (posts):** measured on the sample, a post's median half-width was
17.7 px against 6.0–8.2 for real leaders, so candidate leaders wider than
12 px are skipped. Straightness did **not** separate straight unbranched
trees (1.9 px RMS; real leaders 1.8–6.9 px), so no straightness rule was
added. A trunk-width check was tried and **removed**: ground pixels merge
into the trunk base, so every real trunk looked post-wide.

**Results on the full dataset:**

| Version | Passed QC | Flagged |
|---|---|---|
| v1 | 416 / 541 | 125 |
| v2 | 404 / 541 | 137 |

**Visual inspection of development rows 1, 7 and 9 (by me):** about 45
trees were wrong, *including many that passed QC*. By type:

| Failure | Examples |
|---|---|
| Stops part-way (depth gaps, support wires) | R01N14/22/29/33/34, R07N01–06/10/11 |
| Jumps to another branch or tree | R01N25/31/41/43, R07N04/08, R09N03 |
| Posts (also chosen as the whole tree) | R01N05/08/13/18/32, R07N17/25 |
| Straight unbranched trees | R07N29, R09N18/32 |
| Wrong fork (too late or none) | R01N01/06/35, R07N09/26, R09N12/16 |
| Three main branches | R01N09/14, R09N04 |

On the 17-tree tuning set (`data/tuning/`, problem trees from development
rows only) v2 passed just 5/17.

**Decision:** each fix exposed new failure types, and QC pass rate did not
reflect correctness. Automatic extraction was dropped as the ground-truth
source and kept as a **baseline**, to be scored against the guided ground
truth.

## 3. Guided ground truth (`treeskel.guided_gt`, `treeskel.annotate`)

- **Clicks per tree:** trunk base, fork, left leader tip, right leader tip,
  plus correction clicks (waypoints) where a trace strays. A waypoint is
  added to the nearest trace, in order along it.
- **Cost map:** woodiness =
  0.5 × depth foreground (weighted by centredness within the object)
  + 0.3 × Frangi tube filter (Hessian eigenvalues, σ = 2–10 px)
  + 0.2 × pale-bark colour.
  Cost = 0.02 + (1 − woodiness)².
- **Tracing:** the cheapest 8-connected path between consecutive clicks
  (Dijkstra, `skimage.graph.route_through_array`). The cost map takes about
  0.8 s per tree; tracing is near-instant.
- **Rendering:** widths come from the depth thickness at each point. The
  result is a `WinterGT`, the same type as the baseline, so both methods
  share rendering and overlays and can be compared directly.
- **Click tool:** a matplotlib window. Enter accepts, u undoes, s skips,
  d switches to the depth view, n/p move between trees, q quits. It saves to
  `annotations/winter_clicks.json` after every tree and resumes where it
  left off.
- A synthetic-test finding: the Frangi filter responds at the *edges* of a
  flat-shaded bar. Real branches are shaded like cylinders, so the
  synthetic test tree was shaded that way too.

## 4. Labelling protocol (so far)

- Click points on the branch centreline.
- Tip = the highest visible point of the leader, or where it leaves the top
  of the image.
- Add correction clicks when the trace leaves the wood (grass, sky, posts,
  wires, other trees) or hugs an edge for a long stretch. Small wobbles on
  the wood are left alone.

## 5. First annotation run: the 17 tuning trees

- **Time:** about 6 minutes in total (about 20 s per tree), including
  learning the tool.
- **Corrections:** 64 waypoints (0–11 per tree, mean 3.8). 16 of 17 trees
  needed at least one.

  | Part | Waypoints |
  |---|---|
  | Trunk | 37 |
  | Left leader | 24 |
  | Right leader | 3 |

- **Observed:** the trunk trace runs along the outside of the trunk instead
  of the middle, and leader traces drift off and come back.
- **Likely cause for the trunk:** the depth "centredness" is computed on a
  foreground where ground pixels merge into the trunk base, so the centre is
  off.
- The left vs right imbalance is unexplained. Check whether it comes from
  the trees or from how waypoints are assigned.

## 6. Other changes

- The first full run on Windows stopped with `OSError [Errno 22]`, because
  an output image was open in another program. Both CLIs now skip files
  they cannot write, finish the run, and list those files (`save_image`,
  `report_failed`).
- Dependencies added: scipy, scikit-image, matplotlib.
- Tests: 94, all passing.
- PRs: #10–#14 (winter GT v1, v2, the tuning data via #13, guided GT, the
  locked-file fix).

## 7. AI use in this session (for the reflection)

- **What was mine:**
  - inspecting the overlays and classifying failures (the evidence that
    QC pass rate was misleading);
  - suggesting the post-width rule;
  - identifying straight trees and three-branch trees;
  - deciding to pivot to guided annotation;
  - the two-leader decision (based on the masks);
  - the tip-clicking rule;
  - doing the annotation.
- **AI errors and dead ends:**
  - The AI's "longest path" leader rule and its any-direction gap bridging
    both failed on real images.
  - The trunk-width QC check flagged every tree and was removed.
  - The AI designed v1 while looking at test-row trees (R03, R12). This was
    noted as a limitation, and tuning then moved to development rows only.
  - The AI could not run the GUI itself (no display). It was tested through
    its event handlers, and my first run was the real test.
- **Lesson:** the QC pass count (404/541) looked good, but only visual
  inspection showed how many "passing" trees were wrong. A pass count is
  not an accuracy measure.
